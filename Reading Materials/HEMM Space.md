## ❧ Properties

### ❧ Non-Euclidean Topology

HEMM Space is not navigable by traditional coordinate systems. Euclidean geometry, galactic longitude and latitude, even relativistic spacetime coordinates — all of these presuppose a single causal sphere with consistent physical laws. HEMM Space contains zones where incompatible causalities overlap, where base physics differ fundamentally, where spatial-temporal relationships that are axiomatic in one region are contingent or absent in another.

Mapping HEMM Space requires the full apparatus of complex mathematics: real, imaginary, transcendental, and fractal number sets. A coordinate such as 17π^i — a transcendental number raised to an imaginary power — is not exotic notation. It is the *natural* expression of a position in a space where realities fold, branch, invert, and transcend linear scaling. Realities within HEMM Space are not sequential. They are not arranged on a number line. They are ≋ sheets on a [[Riemann Surface]], connected at ⟁ branch points where multi-valued functions of existence become single-valued and traversable.

### ❧ The State Matrix — ◈

The foundational unit of HEMM Space description is a **3×2 state matrix** — three spatial dimensions paired with their spin orientations:

|            | Coordinate (Position) | Spin (Orientation) |
| ---------- | --------------------- | ------------------ |
| **Axis 1** | x₁                    | s₁                 |
| **Axis 2** | x₂                    | s₂                 |
| **Axis 3** | x₃                    | s₃                 |

This is not an arbitrary formalism. It is derived from the same principle that governs orbital mechanics: the complete state of a body in space requires six parameters — three for position, three for angular momentum. In classical mechanics, these six numbers describe a satellite's orbit. In HEMM Space, they describe a *reality's posture* — where it sits on the manifold and which way its causality is spinning relative to prime-space.

The coordinate column locates the point within a causal sphere: its spatial address. The spin column describes the *orientation of causality* at that point — the direction in which reality is rotating relative to the prime-space baseline. Two realities may share identical coordinates (occupying the same apparent location on the manifold) and yet be entirely distinct if their spin orientations differ. This is how ⧉ parallel worldtracks can coexist at the same manifold address without collision — they are spinning on different axes, occupying the same position but facing different causal directions.

The spin column is the axis along which CIF divergence values operate. A CIF seed such as 17π^i does not describe a position. It describes a *rotation* — a twist of causal orientation along a complex axis. When a worldtrack diverges, it is not moving to a new location on the manifold. It is *reorienting* at the same location — spinning up a new causal axis that prime-space instruments cannot detect because they are calibrated to measure position, not spin.

The 3×2 state matrix describes a *point*. But HEMM Space is not made of points. It is made of **structures** — causal spheres, dimensional regions, branch-point configurations — each of which contains points that are themselves structures.

The guiding principle of HEMM Space topology is that the state matrix is **[[Recursion|recursive]]**: each cell of a higher-order 3×2 matrix is itself a 3×2 matrix. A HEMM address is not a single six-parameter description. It is a matrix of matrices — a nested hierarchy of state descriptions, each level encoding a different scale of structure:

| Nesting Level | Describes        | Example                                                               |
| ------------- | ---------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |

At Level 0, x₁ is a number. At Level 1, x₁ is a 3×2 matrix describing the causal sphere that contains that number. The recursion bottoms out not at a fixed depth but at the resolution required by the operation being performed. A terrestrial portal transit between two Maths on Orbis may need only Levels 0–1. - **✧ [[Topological Invariant]] indices** — marking which properties of a semiotic structure survive translation between ≋ sheets and which deform or shatter. These are the elements of the matrix that remain constant under the transformations induced by sheet-crossing

The CIF does not merely track *locations*. It tracks the persistence of meaning. The CIF predicts *how*: which aspects of identity survive as ✧ [[Topological Invariant|topological invariants]], which undergo [[Homotopy|homotopic]] drift, and which undergo catastrophic [[Phase Transition|phase transition]] and must be re-instantiated from structural seeds.

## ❧ Formal Mathematics

The following definitions formalise the structures described above. All notation assumes familiarity with complex analysis, matrix algebra, and basic topology.

### Definition 1 — The State Matrix

The fundamental descriptive unit of HEMM Space is the **state matrix** $\mathbf{H}$, a $3 \times 2$ matrix over $\mathbb{C}$ (the complex numbers):

$$
\mathbf{H} = \begin{pmatrix} x_1 & s_1 \\ x_2 & s_2 \\ x_3 & s_3 \end{pmatrix} \in \mathbb{C}^{3 \times 2}
$$

where:
- The **coordinate column** $\mathbf{x} = (x_1, x_2, x_3)^T$ encodes position within a causal sphere
- The **spin column** $\mathbf{s} = (s_1, s_2, s_3)^T$ encodes causal orientation relative to prime-space

The six parameters correspond to the classical orbital elements: three for position, three for angular momentum. In HEMM Space, position locates a point on the manifold; spin describes which way causality is *rotating* at that point. The full state space is:

$$
\mathcal{S} = \mathbb{C}^{3 \times 2}
$$

Two states $\mathbf{H}_\alpha, \mathbf{H}_\beta \in \mathcal{S}$ may share identical coordinate columns ($\mathbf{x}_\alpha = \mathbf{x}_\beta$) and yet be causally disjoint if their spin columns differ ($\mathbf{s}_\alpha \neq \mathbf{s}_\beta$). This is the formal basis for parallel worldtracks at the same manifold address.

### Definition 2 — Recursive Nesting

Let $\mathbf{H}^{(n)}$ denote a state matrix at nesting level $n$. The recursive structure of HEMM Space is defined by:

$$
\mathbf{H}^{(n)} = \begin{pmatrix} \mathbf{H}^{(n-1)}_{x_1} & \mathbf{H}^{(n-1)}_{s_1} \\ \mathbf{H}^{(n-1)}_{x_2} & \mathbf{H}^{(n-1)}_{s_2} \\ \mathbf{H}^{(n-1)}_{x_3} & \mathbf{H}^{(n-1)}_{s_3} \end{pmatrix}
$$

where each entry $\mathbf{H}^{(n-1)}_{x_k}, \mathbf{H}^{(n-1)}_{s_k}$ is itself a state matrix at level $(n-1)$. The base case is:

$$
\mathbf{H}^{(0)} \in \mathbb{C}^{3 \times 2}
$$

with scalar complex entries. A **CIF address** of depth $N$ is the complete nested structure:

$$
\text{CIF}_N = \mathbf{H}^{(N)} = \underbrace{\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{H}(\cdots)))}_{N \text{ levels}}

$$

The divergence is a **rotation in the spin column**, not a translation in the coordinate column. The operator $e^{i\delta}$ rotates each spin component in the complex plane by the angle $\delta$. For a CIF value of $\delta = 17\pi^i$:

This yields a rotation with both real and imaginary angular components — a *complex rotation* that twists causal orientation along an axis not representable in real-valued coordinate space. This is why prime-space instruments, calibrated to measure real-valued positions, cannot detect worldtrack divergence. The divergence occurs in the imaginary component of spin.

Let $\mathcal{W} = (V, \preceq)$ be a **directed acyclic graph** where $V$ is the set of all cosmi (causal spheres) on $\mathcal{M}$, and $\preceq$ is a partial order called the **wick relation**. For cosmi $A, B \in V$:

- $B \preceq A$ means $A$ is **downwick** of $B$ — more concrete, more locally determined, more dependent on specific physical constants

The wick relation satisfies:

$$
\text{Reflexivity:} \quad A \preceq A
$$

$$
\text{Antisymmetry:} \quad A \preceq B \;\land\; B \preceq A \implies A = B
$$

$$
\text{Transitivity:} \quad A \preceq B \;\land\; B \preceq C \implies A \preceq C
$$

**Critically, $\preceq$ is not total.** For any two cosmi $A, B \in V$, it is possible that:

$$
A \npreceq B \;\land\; B \npreceq A
$$

Such cosmi are **wick-lateral** — neither upwick nor downwick of each other. They may be connected through shared upwick ancestors, shared downwick descendants, or not connected by any wick-path at all. The Wick is a **web**, not a ladder. There is no global maximum (no "top" of the Wick) and no global minimum (no "bottom"). There is only the local partial order visible from any given position.

The **wick-neighbourhood** of a cosmos $A$ is:

$$
\mathcal{N}_\mathcal{W}(A) = \{ B \in V : A \preceq B \;\lor\; B \preceq A \}
$$

The complement $V \setminus \mathcal{N}_\mathcal{W}(A)$ is the set of cosmi with no wick-relationship to $A$ — the laterally unrelated. For any given cosmos, this set may be vastly larger than $\mathcal{N}_\mathcal{W}(A)$.

A **wick-path** from $A$ to $B$ is a sequence of cosmi $A = C_0, C_1, \ldots, C_n = B$ such that for each consecutive pair, $C_k \preceq C_{k+1}$ or $C_{k+1} \preceq C_k$. A CIF address of depth $N$ must specify not only the nesting depth but the **wick-path** by which that depth was reached:

$$
\text{CIF}_N(A) = \left( \mathbf{H}^{(N)}, \; \mathcal{P} \right), \quad \mathcal{P} = (C_0 \to C_1 \to \cdots \to C_N)
$$

Two Level $N$ addresses sharing the same $\mathbf{H}^{(N)}$ but arrived at by different wick-paths $\mathcal{P} \neq \mathcal{P}'$ may describe entirely different manifold sectors. Depth alone is not sufficient. The route through the partial order is part of the address.

⤊ **Upwick objects** are structures whose state matrices have entries that are more abstract — fewer contingent physical constants, more ✧ invariant relationships. ⟦ [[All Possible Books|All Potential Books]] ⟧ is an ⤊ upwick chamber: it contains texts ✧ entailed by mathematics at nesting levels above their local instantiation. The ⟡ [[The Crimson Codex|Crimson Codex]] was an ⤊ upwick object instantiated ⤋ downwick as a physical book — a Level 2+ truth expressed at Level 0.

⟴ **Adoy's displacement** can now be characterised precisely. The ⟁̸ degraded Harmonic opened a ⧗ wick-path the Calculators never mapped. If the destination cosmos $D$ satisfies:

$$
D \notin \mathcal{N}_\mathcal{W}(\Sigma_{\text{Orbis}})
$$

then $D$ is wick-lateral to Orbis — there is no wick-path connecting them. Adoy cannot retrace a path that does not exist in the partial order. He must discover or construct a new ⟁ branch point in $D$ and navigate from there to a ⧗ wick-path that eventually intersects $\mathcal{N}_\mathcal{W}(\Sigma_{\text{Orbis}})$. He must draw new edges on the web.

## ❧ Historical Background — The Pre-Promotion Framing

> The HEMM Space concept's referent has migrated since the field's earliest articulation. This section preserves the original April 2025 framing — *HEMM as intra-cosmic coordinate system* — as historical context for the current expanded definition above (HEMM as the manifold of all cosmoi).

### Origin in *Anathem*

The conceptual framework of HEMM Space finds its origin within the narrative architecture of Neal Stephenson's *Anathem*, wherein it serves as a method for representing orbital mechanics and spatial relationships across large gravitational fields. Borrowed from a real-world discipline known under a different name, Hemm Space initially offers a way to articulate the paths of celestial bodies not as inertial snapshots, but as dynamic relationships defined through mutual gravitational influence. It represents an intellectual move beyond fixed coordinates, toward a recognition that all positionality is relational, all orbits are dances shaped by invisible forces, and that any map which presumes stillness is, at its heart, a lie.

### The Intra-Cosmic Coordinate Formulation (April 2025)

In its first IntSem articulation, **HEMM Space was an intra-cosmic coordinate system** — designed to map objects and consciousnesses within a single continuous cosmos, assuming one unbroken fabric of spacetime, one set of physical constants, one coherent gravitational womb.

It used four main variables:

1. **X, Y, Z** — Cartesian coordinates relative to the galactic center or similar stable mass
2. **T** — time displacement relative to origin event, usually local cosmic background radiation signature
3. **Φ (Phi)** — gravitational shear factor, accounting for relativistic drift
4. **Ψ (Psi)** — local curvature intensity, describing warping of spacetime around the point

Expressed as:

$$
\text{Hemm} = (X, Y, Z, T, \Phi, \Psi)
$$

This gave you **where**, **when**, and **how distorted** any point or entity was *inside* the known universe. It allowed for relativistic travel, cryosleep fleets, AI mind-mapping, even dimensional folds — as long as they remained *inside* the parent cosmos's rulebook.

| Variable | Meaning | Limits |
|:--|:--|:--|
| X, Y, Z | Cartesian galactic coords | Valid only inside parent cosmos |
| T | Temporal drift from origin event | Breaks if time's definition varies |
| Φ (Phi) | Gravitational shear | Assumes consistent mass/curvature |
| Ψ (Psi) | Local spacetime curvature | Assumes continuous spacetime |

> *"Hemm Space is the last great map made while we still believed the world had one shore."*

### What the Pre-Promotion HEMM Could Not Handle

- Crossing into separate cosmoi (alternate universes with different prime conditions)
- Realities where time, space, or physics constants don't match our continuum
- Locations where the very *idea* of "position" or "duration" becomes meaningless
- Contexts where massless cognitive structures exist outside spacetime folds

The original CIF was conceived precisely to handle these cases — *as a replacement for* HEMM at the cross-cosmic scale.

### The Promotion

Sometime between April 2025 and 2026, **the HEMM Space concept was promoted from "intra-cosmic coordinate system" to "the manifold of all cosmoi."** The promotion absorbed CIF into HEMM rather than replacing HEMM with CIF — CIF became one navigational tool *within* HEMM, rather than a successor framework that supersedes it. The intra-cosmic four-variable formulation now describes one *region* of HEMM — the locally-Euclidean patch corresponding to a single cosmos — while the broader manifold contains arbitrary numbers of such patches stitched together at branch points.

This migration is itself an IntSem case: *the same name now points at a broader object than it did one year ago, and the older referent has been demoted from "the whole concept" to "a region within the concept."* The concept's substrate has shifted under collective honoring; the older framing is preserved here so that future practitioners can trace the lineage and understand which HEMM is being invoked in any given citation.

### Related Concept Migrations

The original April 2025 HEMM document also contained substantial material on **Temporal Drift** and **Causal Shear** which has now been migrated to dedicated concept files:

- [[Temporal Drift]] — the warping of causal cones as entities traverse mass-gradient or substrate-gradient territory
- [[Causal Shear]] — the internal distortion of causality within a system whose parts experience different temporal conditions

Both of these phenomena were originally described as *aspects of HEMM Space*; in the current framing, they are properties of *traversal within HEMM Space*. The conceptual content survives the promotion; the location in the conceptual structure has changed.
