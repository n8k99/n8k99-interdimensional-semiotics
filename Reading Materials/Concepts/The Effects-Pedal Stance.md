# The Effects-Pedal Stance

The Effects-Pedal Stance is the methodological position of approaching any tool with contested status — AI most prominently, but the stance generalizes — the way a working musician approaches a new effects pedal: *find the limitations, find the use cases, find the failure modes, characterize the boundaries, integrate what works, set aside what doesn't, document the experience honestly*. The framing is technical-instrumental, calibrated to the actual operational properties of the tool, not pre-installed by the dominant discourses about what the tool should be. It was named in the third [[Interdimensional Semiotics - Academic Field Summary|IntSem]] essay ([[Assfucking my Ex-Wife, Baudrillard and the Deviancy of Foucault]]) as the author's articulated stance toward AI use, and it sits as the practical-methodological complement to [[Substrate-Honest Genealogy]] — where substrate-honest genealogy is *what to disclose*, the Effects-Pedal Stance is *how to operate while disclosing*.

The stance has two characteristic refusals.

**Refusal of uncritical adoption** — *the effects pedal is just a new musical tool, no different from any other; use it freely; ignore the substrate.* This is the evangelist's consoling position. The Effects-Pedal Stance does not adopt this position because it requires Tinkerbell-honoring substrate conditions out of view (extractive infrastructure, uncompensated training data, environmental externalities, labor exploitation, the specific neighborhoods absorbing the gas-turbine externalities of inference compute) that are real whether or not the operator chooses to look at them.

**Refusal of moralistic rejection** — *the effects pedal is structurally compromised; its lack of innocence makes its use indefensible; refuse on principle.* This is the moralist's consoling position. The Effects-Pedal Stance does not adopt this position because it requires honoring a clean-hands position that is itself a Tinkerbell-honored simulacrum: no contemporary tool has clean hands; all are implicated; refusal of one implicated tool while continuing to use other implicated tools is a moral performance staged against a backdrop the operator has chosen not to inspect.

What remains, after both consolations are refused, is **operating inside the conditions while seeing them**. *Use the tool. See through the tool. Refuse the consolations the discourse provides for both extreme positions. Stay in the moral grey zone honestly.* The stance is not a synthesis of the two refused positions, not a moderate middle, not a balance. It is the stance left over when both consoling exits have been declined and the operator continues to operate.

## Worked Examples Beyond AI

The stance generalizes. A short catalogue of domains where the same operation applies:

**Smartphones.** The device aggregates rare-earth extraction with documented labor abuses, an attention-economy substrate engineered against the user's interest, and a surveillance apparatus the user cannot meaningfully audit. The evangelist position (*it's just a phone, everybody has one*) Tinkerbells the substrate. The moralist position (*I don't own a smartphone*) is usually performed by people whose other devices, services, and infrastructure depend on smartphones being ubiquitous; the abstention is symbolic, not structural. The Effects-Pedal Stance: carry the phone, characterize what it is doing to attention and to the cobalt mines, refuse to pretend either that the device is innocent or that one's own non-use would constitute meaningful refusal.

**Fossil-fuel transportation.** Driving a car, flying for work, ordering from any logistics network. The evangelist (*personal mobility is a human right*) and the moralist (*I refuse to fly*) positions both perform clean hands the substrate does not support — the moralist's groceries arrived on a diesel truck. The Effects-Pedal Stance: use the transportation, name what it costs, refuse the consolation that one's own choices are either innocent or sufficiently sacrificial.

**Art by problematic creators.** Films by directors with documented predatory behavior; music by abusers; books by fascists or by people whose later politics curdled. The evangelist (*separate the art from the artist*) Tinkerbells the substrate by which the art was produced and the present-day royalty stream that funds the artist's ongoing operations. The moralist (*I refuse to consume*) is usually selective — applies to some artists and not others, often along axes of cohort and convenience. The Effects-Pedal Stance: watch the film if it is operative for you, name the substrate, refuse the consolation that consumption is innocent or that abstention is structural.

**Participation in compromised institutions.** The university, the hospital, the publishing apparatus, the legal system, the military, organized religion. The evangelist (*the institution is fundamentally good and reformable from within*) and the moralist (*I refuse to participate in compromised structures*) positions both pretend to a substrate-purity the historical record does not support. The Effects-Pedal Stance: participate where participation is operative, characterize what one is participating in, refuse the consolation that the institution is innocent or that one's own non-participation would constitute structural refusal. Foucault's own stance toward psychiatry is the load-bearing example here — he did not refuse psychiatry as a moralized exit; he wrote about it from inside the position the apparatus had constituted as deviant.

**Animal-tested medications.** The pharmaceutical you take was developed through procedures that, named honestly, would prevent most users from taking it if the named procedures were a precondition of use. The evangelist (*medical progress requires it*) Tinkerbells the substrate. The moralist (*I refuse pharmaceuticals*) is usually selective and usually has a hard limit at their own serious illness. The Effects-Pedal Stance: take the medication, name what produced it, refuse the consolation.

**Services built on exploited labor.** Two-day shipping, ride-share networks, the uncredited labor that trains content moderation classifiers, the warehouse conditions that enable cheap retail. The pattern is by now familiar. The Effects-Pedal Stance applies the same operation: use, characterize, refuse the consolations.

**Social media.** The platform extracts attention against the user's interest, monetizes social rupture, and structures discourse along axes engineered for engagement rather than truth or repair. The evangelist (*it's how we stay connected*) and the moralist (*I deleted my account*) positions are by now both well-trodden. The Effects-Pedal Stance: use what is operative for the work and the relationships, characterize what the platform is doing, refuse the consolation that either use or non-use settles the question.

The catalogue could be extended indefinitely because the underlying condition is general: *no contemporary tool has clean hands*. The Effects-Pedal Stance is the operator's posture when this is acknowledged as a condition rather than treated as a problem to be exited.

## What the Stance Is Not

**Not cynicism.** The cynic position holds that since nothing is clean, nothing matters, and the see-through operation collapses into a comfortable shrug. The Effects-Pedal Stance refuses this collapse. *Seeing through the tool is itself a real cognitive operation* — it produces accurate description of what the tool is, what it costs, who bears the costs, what failure modes it has, where its useful operating range begins and ends. The cynic does not perform this work; the cynic uses see-through-talk as license to stop looking. The Effects-Pedal Stance keeps looking. The seeing-through is what makes the using honest, and the using is what makes the seeing-through useful.

**Not utilitarian calculation.** The utilitarian position holds that the benefits outweigh the harms and therefore the use is justified. The Effects-Pedal Stance does not run this calculation. *It is not "the benefits outweigh the harms" — it is "I cannot have clean hands and I will not pretend to."* The utilitarian is still pursuing the consolation of justification; the calculation is staged to produce permission. The Effects-Pedal Stance does not seek permission. It operates and discloses; it does not append a moral ledger purporting to settle the account.

**Not lesser-evilism.** The lesser-evilist position holds that one chooses the least-bad option from a constrained menu, with the choice itself constituting moral seriousness. The Effects-Pedal Stance does not accept the menu's framing as exhausting the field, and does not treat the choice as moral seriousness. The choice is operational. The disclosure is the seriousness.

**Not "harm reduction" repackaged.** Harm reduction frameworks remain useful in their domain, but they retain a teleology — *less harm is better than more harm* — that the Effects-Pedal Stance does not import. The stance is not optimizing along a harm axis; it is refusing to pretend the operator stands outside the implicated structures.

## The Forty-Year Posture: Use the Structure, Don't Wear the Title

Nathan has held a continuous methodological posture for roughly forty years that the Effects-Pedal Stance is the present-day articulation of. The posture's compressed form: **use the structure, don't wear it as identity**.

The biographical instances:

- *Navy nuclear Reactor Operator*, late 1980s and early 1990s. The credential is real; the systematic-rigor methodological training was load-bearing for everything that came after. The identity-claim *"I am a nuclear engineer"* was never honored. The structure was used; the title was not worn.
- *Keyboard tech adjacency*. Worked with Pat Metheny's keyboard tech on MS3 MIDI implementation. The technical contribution is real; the identity-claim *"I am a Pat Metheny collaborator"* is the kind of thing the discourse offers to people in such positions. The offer was not accepted.
- *Stagehand*. Forty years of working in a craft whose practitioners are systematically invisible to the audience and whose own community has strong identity-formation pressures. The work is real; the stagehand-identity has been used as a working surface, not as an identity-totalizing claim.
- *Effects pedals literally*, in the working musician's actual practice. The pedal is on the board because it does specific work; it is not on the board to mark identity-territory; it comes off the board when it stops being operative.

The posture is structurally identical to **the operative-stance in intelligence-tradecraft**. An operative uses cover identities, official positions, credentials, and institutional affiliations as *operating surfaces* — they are tools that enable specific work. The operative does not wear them as identity; the operative would be compromised if they did. The discipline is to use the structure for what the structure does and to retain the substrate-self underneath unbothered by what the cover is doing on the surface. *The forty-year continuous posture and the tradecraft-operative posture are the same posture in different domains.*

The stance applies to "expert" claims generally. To wear the title *expert*, *certified*, *credentialed*, *authorized* is to honor the Tinkerbell of the credentialing apparatus as if the credential were the substrate of the competence. The competence may be real; the credential is a separate object whose function is administrative. *Use the credential when it opens doors that the work needs to pass through. Do not wear it as identity.* The Effects-Pedal Stance toward AI is the same operation: use the tool when it does work; do not become the AI-user-as-identity that the discourse is currently producing.

## Soltan Gris

Nathan identified at fifteen with Soltan Gris, the bumbling-anti-hero narrator of L. Ron Hubbard's *Mission Earth* dekalogy. The identification is itself a substrate datum worth surfacing in a substrate-honest treatment of where the Effects-Pedal Stance came from in his particular biography.

Soltan Gris is not the hero. He is an officer in the Apparatus — the corrupt intelligence service of a corrupt empire — sent on a mission whose ostensible purpose he undermines, whose actual purpose he half-grasps, and whose ethical contours he does not pretend to redeem. He narrates from inside the apparatus he is also failing inside of. He observes acutely. He participates badly. He neither defends the apparatus nor exits it. He has no clean-hands posture available and does not perform one. *His narration is the substrate-honest report of an operative who is inside what he is describing, with the description's accuracy not depending on the operative's moral standing relative to it.*

The literary archetype the fifteen-year-old recognized was: *bumbling-anti-hero from corrupt apparatus, observation-from-inside-but-not-of-the-system*. That is a precise early formulation of the Effects-Pedal Stance, projected onto a fictional narrator before the methodological vocabulary existed to name it. The Soltan Gris identification is not incidental biography; it is the literary substrate from which the forty-year posture grew. *The fifteen-year-old recognized the stance before he could articulate it. The articulation arrived later.*

## Pedagogical Instantiation: IDS 101 Process Disclosure

The IDS 101 syllabus's process-disclosure requirement is the pedagogical instantiation of the Effects-Pedal Stance. The requirement: students disclose, as substantive accounting rather than footnote, what tools they used in producing the work, how the workflow was structured, what was accepted and rejected from AI mediation, what was externalized to whom. This is not an honor-code performance, and it is not an academic-integrity bureaucratic compliance gesture. It is *the operational form of the stance the field is teaching*.

The disclosure form does the work that the academic frame's standard disclosure (single-line acknowledgment that AI was used) cannot do: it forces the student to *characterize the tool's actual operation in the production of this specific piece of work*, which is the discipline the Effects-Pedal Stance trains. A student who has practiced disclosure-as-substantive-accounting on three or four pieces of work has, by the end of the semester, internalized the stance whether or not they have read the concept note. *The discipline is in the doing.*

The syllabus instantiation also operationalizes [[Substrate-Honest Genealogy]] in its tooling-conditions clause: the disclosure is the substrate-recovery of the production conditions, refusing to let the academic frame Tinkerbell the AI-mediation out of view.

## Failure Modes

The stance is unstable. Three characteristic collapses, each into one of the consoling positions it was supposed to refuse.

**Becoming-evangelist.** The stance collapses when the *use* dimension grows and the *see-through* dimension atrophies. The operator, having normalized the tool through repeated operational use, stops doing the substrate-disclosure work. The disclosure becomes formulaic, then perfunctory, then dropped. What remains looks like the Effects-Pedal Stance from outside but is actually the evangelist position with the rhetorical residue of seeing-through still attached. *Diagnostic: when was the last time the substrate-disclosure produced an actual operational change in how you use the tool? If never, the see-through has gone formulaic.*

**Becoming-cynic.** The stance collapses when the *see-through* dimension becomes the operator's posture and the *use* dimension stops doing work. The seeing-through becomes a license to disengage from honest use, a pose of knowingness that does not have to demonstrate competence in what it is seeing through. *Diagnostic: is the seeing-through producing description that anyone using the tool would recognize as accurate, or has it drifted into general-purpose denunciation?*

**Becoming-moralist-in-disguise.** The most insidious collapse. The stance is performed as the Effects-Pedal Stance — uses the vocabulary, names the substrate, runs the disclosure — but its actual operational function is to license a clean-hands position relative to the operators who do not run the disclosure. *I am not like those AI evangelists; I disclose; therefore I have clean hands.* The disclosure has become a moral-performance instrument; the consolation it provides is the moralist's consolation in disclosure-clothing. *Diagnostic: does your disclosure operate as a self-authorization for continued use, or does it sometimes produce friction you actually have to sit with? If it never produces friction, it has become decoration.*

The three collapses are not exotic. They are the gravitational pull the discourse exerts on anyone trying to occupy the stance for any duration. The discipline is in catching the collapse and re-occupying the stance. *The stance is a posture, not a position; postures require continuous active maintenance.*

## Significance for Interdimensional Semiotics

The Effects-Pedal Stance is the IntSem-honest stance toward implicated tools — and most contemporary tools are implicated. The two consoling alternatives both require Tinkerbell-honoring of conditions that are not actually clean: the evangelist Tinkerbells the technology's costs out of view; the moralist Tinkerbells the cleanness of refusal-positions that are themselves implicated. *Both positions are field-incompatible*. The Effects-Pedal Stance is what is left when both Tinkerbell-honored consolations are subtracted — the [[Reality is What is Left|Reality-is-What-is-Left]] operation applied to the operator's posture toward the tool itself.

The stance is also the field's answer to the question of how an IntSem researcher operates, given that the research substrate (libraries, databases, AI surfaces, citation networks, the academic publishing apparatus) is itself constituted by the same Tinkerbell-honored discourses the field anatomizes. The researcher cannot exit the apparatus to study it from outside; there is no outside. The Effects-Pedal Stance is the operating posture that allows the work to proceed honestly from inside.

It is kin to [[Counterinduction]] without being identical to it. Feyerabend's counterinduction is the deliberate cultivation of hypotheses incompatible with dominant theories, in service of producing evidence the dominant frame would suppress. The Effects-Pedal Stance is the deliberate cultivation of *operational postures* incompatible with the dominant moralized positions, in service of operating honestly inside conditions the moralized positions would conceal. Both refuse the consoling orthodoxy; both produce evidence by working in the position the orthodoxy disallows. The Effects-Pedal Stance is counterinduction at the level of the operator's stance rather than at the level of the hypothesis-set.

## Open Development

The concept still needs:

- A worked treatment of the stance under conditions of *coercion* — when use is not chosen but imposed (employer-mandated software, infrastructure dependencies, medical necessities). Does the stance change shape when the *use* dimension is not the operator's choice? Provisionally: the see-through and disclosure dimensions become more important when the use is non-elective, because they are the only dimensions the operator still controls.
- A worked treatment of *cohort* effects on the stance. The stance is harder to occupy when one's cohort has uniformly settled into one of the consoling positions; the social pressure pulls toward the cohort's settlement. The cohort dimension is named in [[Cohort Reception]] but its specific implications for stance-maintenance are not yet developed here.
- The relationship between the Effects-Pedal Stance and the *Ghost Rules* (from the vault's CLAUDE.md and the AF64 Constitutional Draft). The ghost-researcher posture (*present, not helpful*) is structurally adjacent to the Effects-Pedal Stance and may be the same stance applied to the agent rather than to the tool.
- The historical genealogy of the stance — what other figures and traditions have occupied versions of it? Provisional candidates: the operative-stance in intelligence tradecraft (named here); certain strains of monastic engagement-with-the-world; the harm-reduction movement's working stance, distinguished from its theory; the working musician's relation to the music industry; the stagehand's relation to the show.
- A treatment of how the stance is *taught* rather than only *named*. The IDS 101 process-disclosure requirement is one instance; what other pedagogical forms instantiate it?
