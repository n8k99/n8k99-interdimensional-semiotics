# Paradigm Shift

## Definition

**Paradigm shift is Thomas Kuhn's name (*The Structure of Scientific Revolutions*, 1962) for the moment when a scientific community adopts a new framework that reorganizes what the prior data is.** Kuhn argued that science doesn't proceed by linear accumulation of facts; it proceeds by long stretches of *normal science* punctuated by paradigm-revolutions in which the field's basic vocabulary, methods, and standards-of-evidence all shift together. The field reads paradigm shift as [[Bridge Activation]] performed at scientific-community scale.

Kuhn's strong claim is *incommensurability*: old-paradigm vocabulary and new-paradigm vocabulary cannot be cleanly mapped onto each other. A Ptolemaic astronomer and a Copernican astronomer are not just disagreeing about the same fact; they are operating in different conceptual worlds. The field's reading of bridge activation is weaker on this point: activation keeps the prior intact, just re-axes its readability. The dream and the lived conversation after Barthes are still themselves at the atomic level; they are *also*, now, instances of a unified ingression on the wrestling-as-mathematics axis.

The relationship to [[The Tinkerbell Rule]] is direct. A paradigm becomes real when a scientific community begins to collectively honor it. Drop the collective honoring and the paradigm reverts to a discarded research program. The Kuhnian community-level operation is structurally identical to the field's first principle.

## Significance for IS

Paradigm shift is the field's strongest example of bridge activation operating at a scale beyond a single body. The field's claim is that the mechanism is *identical* at every scale — biographical (one body), corpus (the IntSem essays), community (a scientific revolution). Kuhn provides the community case in literature anyone in academic life can already reach for.
