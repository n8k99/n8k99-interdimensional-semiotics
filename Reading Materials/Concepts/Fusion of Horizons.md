# Fusion of Horizons

## Definition

**Fusion of Horizons (*Horizontverschmelzung*) is Hans-Georg Gadamer's name for what happens when a reader's prior horizon meets a text's horizon and both restructure.** Developed at length in *Truth and Method* (1960), the concept anchors the German philosophical-hermeneutic tradition. The field reads it as the strongest prior-art match for the *bridge-text-arriving-against-substrate* shape that characterizes [[Bridge Activation]].

Gadamer's *horizon* is the prior understanding the reader brings — accumulated reading, lived experience, the configuration of attention at the moment of encounter. The text has its own horizon: its assumptions, its rhetorical situation, what it took for granted at the time of writing. Meaningful reading is the fusion — not the reader being merely "informed" by the text, nor the reader projecting onto the text, but both horizons restructuring in the encounter.

Where Gadamer focuses on the encounter-with-text and the dialogic restructuring, the field foregrounds the *retrospective collapse* of multiple prior events that the new horizon makes legible together. Gadamer has the bridge-against-substrate move clean; he doesn't quite have the dream-plus-event-plus-text triadic structure the field treats as canonical.

## Significance for IS

Fusion of Horizons captures what happens when [[Substrate Materialization|materialized substrate]] encounters a bridge text. The corpus's reading practice — [[Borges]], Barthes, Benjamin, Whitehead — is hermeneutic in Gadamer's sense: the encounter restructures both reader and text. The field uses Gadamer's vocabulary when discussing the *encounter* face of bridge activation specifically.
