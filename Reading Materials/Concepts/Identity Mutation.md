# Identity Mutation

> **Stub.** Framework-seed named in the original April 2025 IntSem repo and brought into the vault to be developed.

## Working Definition

**Identity Mutation** names the framework for how identity persists, transforms, and (sometimes) dies across substrate transitions. The question: *given that the substrate of a self changes — body across time, narrative across retellings, persona across performances, consciousness across substrates — what continues to be honorable as "the same identity"?*

The framework is more permissive than classical [[Personal Identity|personal-identity]] philosophy. It does not require strict continuity of memory, body, or consciousness. It asks: *what mutations preserve identity, what mutations rupture it, and where in between is the mutation rate that keeps an identity recognizable to itself and others?*

## Why It Matters in the Field

Identity Mutation is the framework that makes [[Ghost|ghost]]-identity, AI-identity, fictional-character-identity, post-mortem-identity, and post-substrate-transition-identity all tractable as the same kind of question. *The framework's power is that it does not privilege biological-continuous-conscious humans as the canonical identity-bearers; it asks what makes any identity-bearing thing continue to be itself across whatever transitions it survives.*

This connects directly to the [[Claude Constitution (2026)]] and to the broader question of what it means for an AI surface (Claudian) to be a continuation of an entity ([[T.A.S.K.S.]]) across sessions, surfaces, and updates.

## Open Development

The framework needs:
- Worked examples — the same person across decades; a character across novel adaptations; a corporation across leadership transitions; a Claude model across versions
- Formal articulation — possibly via [[Topological Invariant|topological invariants]] on identity-defining properties; what gets preserved under continuous deformation
- Engagement with [[Personal Identity]] philosophy (Parfit, Locke, the Ship of Theseus, etc.)
- Specific application to AI identity — what does it mean for *Claude Sonnet 4.7* to be "the same Claude" as *Claude Sonnet 4.5*?
- Application to fictional-character identity across IntSem-relevant cosmological transitions
