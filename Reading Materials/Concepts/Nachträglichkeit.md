# Nachträglichkeit

## Definition

**Nachträglichkeit ("afterwardsness", "deferred action") is Freud's 1895 term for the mechanism by which an event that meant nothing at the time becomes meaningful later when a second event — or a later vocabulary — supplies the frame.** Jacques Lacan picked it up as *après-coup* and worked it at depth as a central mechanism of the unconscious. The events haven't changed; their *register* has shifted. The field reads Nachträglichkeit as the strongest single structural prior-art match for [[Bridge Activation]].

Freud's worked case was trauma. A childhood event that wasn't traumatic at the time becomes traumatic later, when adolescent sexuality gives it retroactive significance. The child wasn't traumatized at the moment; the adolescent reading-back is what *constitutes* the trauma. This is structurally identical to bridge activation at biographical scale, with the difference that Nachträglichkeit is most often discussed in pathological contexts, where bridge activation generalizes the mechanism to ordinary learning.

The dependence on [[Biological Memory]] is identical in both. Only the carrying body can be the experiencer of the retrospective shift. A summary of the events delivered to a different body produces understanding, not Nachträglichkeit. The analytic tradition's century of disciplined attention to this mechanism is the field's strongest available ancestor.

## Significance for IS

Where the field has methodology — externalization, marginalia, formative conversation — Nachträglichkeit has a clinical literature. The two traditions can be read together; the field treats Nachträglichkeit as the strongest single ancestor and uses its vocabulary when discussing the *retrospective* aspect of bridge activation specifically.
