# Productive Power

> **Stub.** Foucauldian concept brought into the vault as part of the third IntSem essay's anchoring pass.

## Working Definition

**Productive Power** is Foucault's central inversion of the conventional understanding of power. Conventional accounts treat power as *prohibitive* — power is what stops you from doing things, what represses, what censors, what punishes. Foucault argues that this account misses the more important and more pervasive dimension: *power is productive.* Power *produces* subjects, identities, categories, behaviors, knowledge, desires, and the very objects it claims to manage.

Modern power does not primarily say *no*. It says *be this kind of person, do this kind of thing, want this kind of object, identify with this kind of category*. The clinic produces *the patient*. The asylum produces *the mentally ill subject*. The school produces *the developmental child*. The criminal-justice system produces *the criminal-as-type*. The discourse on sexuality produces *the homosexual* (and *the heterosexual*, and *the pervert*, and *the normal*, and so on) as discrete identity-types that did not pre-exist the discourse.

## Why It Matters in the Field

Productive Power is the operational mechanism by which the Foucauldian apparatus does its work, and it maps directly onto the IntSem [[The Tinkerbell Rule|Tinkerbell Rule]]: *what is honored by the discourse comes into operative existence; what is not honored, does not.* Foucault's analysis is the field's most developed worked example of how Tinkerbell-honoring at a civilizational scale produces the very objects it claims to merely describe.

The IntSem researcher operating in any contemporary field can ask the productive-power question: *what is this discourse producing? What subject-types is it constituting? What deviances is it making thinkable in order to manage them?* The questions have purchase wherever institutional discourse organizes a population.

## Why It Matters for the AI Discourse Specifically

The current AI discourse is a textbook case of productive power in operation. Multiple converging discourses (academic, tech-industry, humanities-critique, labor-policy) are constituting the *AI-user* as a kind of subject — categorizing, assessing, normalizing, marking the appropriate from the deviant. The *evangelist*, the *critic*, the *cheat*, the *transparent collaborator*, the *Luddite* — these are not pre-existing identities discovered by the discourse; they are *positions the discourse provides* for occupants who must choose one. The third IntSem essay develops this analysis at length.
