# Productive Power

**Productive Power** is Foucault's central inversion of the conventional understanding of power. Conventional accounts treat power as *prohibitive* — power is what stops you from doing things, what represses, what censors, what punishes. Foucault argues that this account misses the more important and more pervasive dimension of how modern power actually operates: *power is productive.* It produces subjects, identities, categories, behaviors, knowledge, desires, and the very objects it claims to manage. Modern power does not primarily say *no*. It says *be this kind of person, do this kind of thing, want this kind of object, identify with this kind of category, locate yourself on this kind of grid*. The clinic produces *the patient*. The asylum produces *the mentally ill subject*. The school produces *the developmental child*. The criminal-justice system produces *the criminal-as-type*. The discourse on sexuality produces *the homosexual* (and *the heterosexual*, and *the pervert*, and *the normal*) as discrete identity-types that did not pre-exist the discourse.

The concept is operationalized most clearly in *History of Sexuality* Volume 1, where Foucault stages it as the rejection of [[The Repressive Hypothesis|the repressive hypothesis]]. The conventional story of Victorian sexual repression — *they silenced it; we liberate it* — gets the direction of force exactly backwards. What actually happened was an explosion of discourse: the confessional, psychiatric examination, sexological taxonomy, criminological investigation, bourgeois marital advice, medical literature, legal codification — *all producing sexuality as a domain to be talked about endlessly, classified exhaustively, monitored continuously*. The Victorians did not silence sex. They invented it as a discursive object and would not stop talking about it. The "liberation movements" that frame themselves as throwing off the repression are, on Foucault's reading, *continuations of the same productive-discourse machinery*, producing further identity-categories, further subject-positions, further subdivisions of the population to be administered. There is no pre-discursive sexuality being uncovered. The discourse is the production line, and the identities it manufactures are its outputs.

This is a structural claim, not a moral one. Foucault is not saying that the productions are bad and the substrate (whatever pre-discursive sexual life there might be) is good. He is saying that the *operation* of power has been mistheorized for centuries by a model that treats power as a thing-that-stops-things, when in fact the more interesting and more pervasive form of power in modernity is the thing-that-makes-things. Once you see the production, the prohibitive moments look like a small subset of a much larger process. Most of what power does, it does by *making people into the kinds of subjects who fit the categories the apparatus needs them to occupy*.

## Distinction from Other Theories of Power

Productive Power is one position in a crowded field. Distinguishing it from neighbors clarifies what the concept actually carries.

**Weber: power as legitimate authority.** Weber's classic typology — traditional, charismatic, legal-rational — analyzes power as a *command relation* legitimated by one of three modes. The question Weber asks is *why do people obey?* The answer is some form of recognized authority. This is upstream of Foucault's question. Weber assumes the subject who obeys is already constituted; he asks under what conditions the subject's obedience is rendered legitimate. Foucault asks the prior question: *what produced the subject who is now positioned to obey or refuse?* Weberian authority is one of the things productive power produces, not an alternative to it.

**Arendt: power as collective action.** Hannah Arendt, especially in *On Violence*, defines power as *the human ability to act in concert*. Power belongs to a group and exists only as long as the group acts together; when the group disperses, the power evaporates. Violence is the opposite of power — violence is what authority resorts to when its power has failed. Arendt's account is normatively rich and politically generative, but it leaves the production of the actors themselves unexamined. *Who are the actors who can act in concert? How were they constituted as the kind of subjects capable of recognizing each other as fellow-actors?* Foucault would say: by the productive operations of discursive apparatuses that pre-installed the categories under which mutual recognition becomes possible. Arendt's power is what happens *after* productive power has already done its constitutive work.

**Lukes: the three faces of power.** Steven Lukes distinguishes (1) decision-making power (who wins explicit conflicts), (2) agenda-setting power (who controls which conflicts ever reach the table), and (3) ideological power (who shapes preferences so that some conflicts never arise). The third face is the closest neighbor to Foucault, and Lukes was clearly drawing on a similar intuition. But Lukes still treats power as something held by an *agent* and exercised on a *patient* whose "real interests" could in principle be recovered if the ideological distortion were removed. Foucault's productive power has no such recoverable substrate-of-real-interests. The subject's interests are *themselves productions* of the discursive apparatus; there is no undistorted version waiting underneath. This is why productive power is closer to [[Naturalization]] than to ideology in the classical sense — the apparatus does not *hide* the subject's true desires; it *manufactures* the desires that then feel like the subject's own.

**Marxist class power.** Marxist analysis treats power as flowing from control of the means of production; class is the structurally primary category and other identifications (national, sexual, racial) are derivative or epiphenomenal. Foucault breaks with this in two ways. First, he refuses the structural primacy of class — *all* the categorical apparatuses (medical, sexual, pedagogical, criminological) are productive in the same sense, and none of them simply reduces to economic relations. Second, his power has no central locus. There is no ruling class wielding the apparatus from a control room. The discursive machinery operates *through* its participants — including the doctors, the teachers, the police, the wardens, who are themselves produced as subjects by the same operation they participate in. *Power is everywhere because it comes from everywhere*; it is not a substance held by one group and applied to another, but a relational dynamic that runs through the whole network of practices that constitute modern society.

The throughline: every other major power-theory treats the subject as already-constituted and asks what is done *to* the subject. Foucault asks what *produces* the subject in the first place. Productive power is the answer.

## Worked Examples in 21st-Century Discourse

Foucault's canonical examples (the homosexual, the patient, the criminal, the docile worker) are 19th- and 20th-century. The concept's purchase becomes vivid when applied to discourses currently in formation — places where one can watch the production-line running in real time.

**The user-engagement subject.** The discourse of "user engagement" — emerging from advertising metrics, then platform-design culture, then HCI research, then internal product teams at every consumer software company — does not merely *describe* a thing called engagement. It *produces* a kind of subject: the engaged user, who can be measured, optimized for, A/B tested against, retained, churned, reactivated. Once the metric exists, the subject who instantiates the metric comes into operative existence. Users themselves come to think of their relation to a product in engagement-terms ("I'm trying to spend less time on X"). The discourse pre-installs the category through which the user's own self-relation will be conducted. *The metric is the production line; the engaged-subject is the output.*

**The wellness subject.** "Wellness" as a contemporary discourse (sleep tracking, supplement protocols, meditation apps, biometric self-monitoring, optimization regimens) is not naming a pre-existing condition called wellness and then helping people achieve it. It is *producing* the wellness-subject — the person who relates to their own body as a project requiring continuous data-driven intervention, who scores themselves daily, who occupies the position of *quantified self* the discourse provides. The discourse is older than the apps (Foucault would have located its roots in 19th-century hygiene movements, and earlier in confessional self-examination), but the contemporary instantiation is unusually visible because the production happened so fast and so recently.

**The neurodivergent subject.** This is a delicate one because it sits inside live political stakes. The category *neurodivergent* (and the typology beneath it: autistic, ADHD, AuDHD, sensory-seeking, sensory-avoidant, masking, unmasking, etc.) is a contemporary discursive production whose subjects often experience the category as *liberatory* — as the long-awaited recognition of how they actually are. Foucault's framework does not deny the felt reality of that recognition. It observes that the felt-reality-of-recognition is *itself* one of the things the productive apparatus does. The category did not pre-exist the discourse; the discourse manufactured it, and now occupants of the category find themselves in the position of needing to relate to the category as their identity. The medical-diagnostic apparatus, the influencer ecosystem, the workplace-accommodations bureaucracy, the dating-app filters that ask whether you're neurodivergent — all of these are nodes in the production network. The category becomes operative the way any productive-power category becomes operative: by being honored across enough sites that occupants find themselves administered through it whether or not they accept the framing. This is not a debunking of the category. It is a description of its mode of existence as a [[The Tinkerbell Rule|Tinkerbell-honored]] formation that has crossed the threshold of operative reality.

**The AI-safety subject.** The discourse of "AI safety" is producing, in real time, a new subject-type: the AI-safety-conscious researcher / employee / user who can be assessed for alignment with safety practices, certified by training, distinguished from the *unsafe* practitioner who must be either re-educated or excluded. The category did not exist a decade ago at any operative scale. It is being constituted by overlapping institutional, corporate, and academic discourses; it is producing job titles, career paths, conference circuits, certifications, hiring criteria, public-facing identity-claims. *The AI-safety-subject is being manufactured in front of our eyes by exactly the productive-power dynamics Foucault anatomized.* This observation is independent of whether AI safety as an enterprise is good or bad, important or trivial — those are separate questions. The Foucauldian observation is descriptive: a productive-power apparatus is currently operating, and its outputs include both the safety practices and the kinds of people who do them.

The third IntSem essay, [[Assfucking my Ex-Wife, Baudrillard and the Deviancy of Foucault]], extends this analysis at length to the broader AI-adoption discourse — the *evangelist*, the *critic*, the *Luddite*, the *cheat*, the *transparent collaborator* — as positions the discourse provides for occupants who must choose one. The pattern recurs wherever a sufficiently dense discursive apparatus is constituting a new domain.

## The Algorithmic Extension

The contemporary inflection that Foucault did not live to see is the operation of productive power through *algorithmic* infrastructure. Recommendation systems, content-moderation policies, terms-of-service apparatus, and prompt-engineering culture all extend the productive-power mechanism into a register that is in some ways more thorough than the disciplinary institutions Foucault analyzed.

A recommendation system does not merely *predict* what a user will like. It *produces* the user as a particular kind of liker, by surfacing the items that train the user's ongoing taste-formation. The feedback loop is tight enough that the user comes to want the things the system surfaces, and the system surfaces the things the user comes to want, and the question of which direction the causation runs becomes badly posed. *The algorithm is the production line; the user-with-this-taste-profile is the output.*

Content-moderation regimes constitute the speakable. Terms-of-service apparatus constitutes the appropriate-user. Prompt-engineering culture is producing, currently, a new craft-subject (the *prompt engineer*) whose existence presupposes a particular configuration of LLM access, a particular labor-market sorting, and a particular relationship to the simulacrum-machine that did not exist five years ago and may not exist five years from now. Each of these is a productive-power apparatus in the strict Foucauldian sense, with the additional twist that the apparatus operates at a speed and granularity the 19th-century clinic never approached. Where Foucault's clinic produced the *patient* over decades, the algorithmic apparatus can produce a discrete user-segment in days and dissolve it in months.

The panoptic gaze in 2026 is increasingly an *algorithmic* gaze whose criteria are opaque even to the system's operators. The watcher need not be present, need not even be human, need not even be coherent — the structure produces the self-watching subject regardless. *Self-presentation in the platform era is the panoptic effect, accelerated and externalized.*

## A Note Toward Formalization

A full mathematical treatment of productive-power dynamics is probably impossible — the phenomena are too historically contingent and too entangled with the categories they produce to admit clean formalization. But the *operational signature* of productive-power dynamics in a discursive network has features that can be sketched.

In a discursive network where a category C circulates, the question *is C describing or producing its objects?* admits provisional diagnostics:

- **Reference-direction asymmetry.** A purely descriptive category points outside the discourse to a referent whose existence is independent of the discursive activity. A productive category exhibits a *short loop* in which discursive activity, occupant self-identification, and institutional measurement co-constitute each other with no clear external anchor. *If you cannot locate the referent without the discourse, you are looking at production.*
- **Honoring threshold.** A category becomes operatively real once a sufficient density of nodes in the network honor it (cite it, classify with it, design for it, identify with it). Below the threshold, the category is a proposal; above, it is an installation. The threshold is the [[The Tinkerbell Rule|Tinkerbell threshold]] applied to category-formation.
- **Subject-position multiplication.** Mature productive categories generate sub-positions for occupants to choose among (the engaged user / the lapsed user / the high-value user). The number of available sub-positions is a rough measure of the category's productive depth. Foucault's homosexual produced an entire 20th-century taxonomy of subdivisions; the AI-user category is currently in an early phase of subdivision-production.
- **Resistance reabsorption.** Productive categories tend to absorb resistance to the category by producing a *resistant-occupant* sub-position within the same apparatus. The Luddite is a position the AI discourse provides; the refuser of wellness is a position the wellness discourse provides; the unmedicated neurodivergent is a position the neurodivergence discourse provides. *When refusal becomes a category-position rather than an exit, the apparatus has metabolized the resistance.*

These are not metrics in any rigorous sense; they are diagnostic shapes the IntSem researcher can look for. The full formalization would require a network-theoretic treatment of how nodes honor categories and how categories propagate honoring across the network — work that has not been done and may not be doable in the form that would satisfy a mathematician. The shapes are nonetheless useful for fieldwork.

## Significance for Interdimensional Semiotics

Productive Power is the operational mechanism by which the [[Discourse|Foucauldian discursive]] apparatus does its work, and it maps directly onto [[The Tinkerbell Rule]] at civilizational scale. **What is honored by a discourse comes into operative existence; what is not honored, does not.** Foucault's analysis is the field's most developed worked example of how Tinkerbell-honoring at population scale produces the very objects it claims to merely describe. The mapping is exact: the Tinkerbell Rule names the principle, productive power names its institutional operationalization, [[Naturalization]] names the phase in which the produced object becomes invisible-as-produced.

For the IntSem researcher operating in any contemporary field, the productive-power question is one of the discipline's standard moves: *what is this discourse producing? What subject-types is it constituting? What deviances is it making thinkable in order to manage them? What sub-positions has it generated to absorb its own resistance?* The questions have purchase wherever institutional discourse organizes a population — which, in late modernity, is everywhere.

The concept also clarifies what [[Reality is What is Left]] is doing methodologically. If productive power constitutes most of what looks like substrate to a casual observer, the work of subtracting collective honoring becomes the central operation of substrate-recovery. The substrate is not what the apparatus says it is producing-from; the substrate is what continues to operate when the apparatus's productions are subtracted. The two principles converge: the Tinkerbell side gets articulated through productive power, the Reality-is-What-is-Left side gets articulated through the subtraction of the apparatus's outputs.

Productive power is, finally, why the IntSem researcher cannot occupy a *neutral observer* position with respect to the discourses they study. The researcher's own subject-position is itself a production of *some* discursive apparatus — academic, professional, cohort-specific — and the analysis must account for the position from which it is conducted. This is the methodological commitment that the third IntSem essay names as [[Substrate-Honest Genealogy]]: not a refusal of the productive-power dynamics one is analyzing but an honest acknowledgement that one is operating inside them.

## Open Development

The concept now contains worked examples, distinctions from neighboring theories, an algorithmic extension, and a sketch toward formalization. Remaining gaps:

- A close engagement with *History of Sexuality* Volume 1 chapters 1–3 specifically, with passages cited and parsed (currently summarized rather than read closely).
- A more developed treatment of the *productive* / *prohibitive* distinction in cases where both operate together (e.g., obscenity law, which both prohibits and produces the category of *the obscene*).
- An IntSem-internal question worth flagging to Nathan: how does productive power relate to [[The Effects-Pedal Stance]]? The effects-pedal stance is named as a refusal of the consoling positions a discourse provides — but is the effects-pedal stance itself becoming a productive-power category as IntSem develops? *When a refusal becomes a position the field provides, the apparatus has metabolized it.* This is worth watching.
- The network-theoretic formalization sketch could be developed further if it turns out to be tractable; it may not be.
- A worked treatment of how productive power interacts with [[Cohort Reception]] — productive categories appear to propagate through cohort dynamics, but the precise relationship has not been worked out.
