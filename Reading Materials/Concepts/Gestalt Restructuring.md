# Gestalt Restructuring

## Definition

**Gestalt restructuring (*Umstrukturierung*) is the early-20th-century Gestalt psychology tradition's name for the discontinuous moment of recognition in which a problem-space reorganizes itself.** Wolfgang Köhler's 1925 *The Mentality of Apes* gave the canonical example: chimpanzees presented with a banana too high to reach and scattered tools "suddenly see" how to stack the boxes or use the stick. The solution is not arrived at by trial and error; the problem-space restructures, and the solution becomes visible. Max Wertheimer's *Productive Thinking* (1945) generalized the move beyond animal cognition to human problem-solving.

The field reads Gestalt restructuring as the psychology of the *firing* of [[Bridge Activation]] — the felt, observable moment when the prior substrate and the delivered vocabulary collapse onto a unified axis. The marginalia note typed at the moment of activation is the recording of a restructuring event in the Gestalt sense. The chimpanzee's *aha* and the experiencer's *this-is-the-bridge* are the same kind of event.

Where the Gestalt tradition focuses on single problem-solving episodes, the field treats the same mechanism as biographical accumulation. The firing in Köhler's chimp is structurally the same as the firing in the experiencer reading Barthes against a thirteen-day-old dream; the difference is scale and the role of prior substrate-laying.

## Significance for IS

Gestalt restructuring provides the psychological literature on the *moment* of bridge activation. It does not have the methodology for cultivating substrate or for distinguishing provision from activation, but it has decades of careful description of what the felt event is like. The field's use of marginalia as the canonical residue of activation aligns with Gestalt's emphasis on the discontinuity of the moment.
