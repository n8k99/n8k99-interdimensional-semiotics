# Pitch Glow

**Pitch Glow** is the temporary semiotic force a concept gains from the conditions of presentation rather than from its tested ability to survive substrate contact.

It appears when a symbol, world premise, product claim, narrative mechanic, or institutional doctrine feels more real because the room, deck, prototype, authority figure, visual polish, or cohort mood honors it intensely. Pitch Glow is Tinkerbell-real while the honoring holds. It becomes dangerous when mistaken for [[Semiotic Mass]], [[Semiotic Resilience]], or actual implementability.

## Definition

A concept is under Pitch Glow when:

- its apparent strength depends heavily on presentation conditions;
- the audience response arrives before substrate testing;
- objections are treated as failure of imagination rather than contact with reality;
- the concept loses coherence when moved into production, play, governance, or repeated use;
- the residue left after the honoring is subtracted is thinner than the pitch implied.

Pitch Glow is not deception by default. It can be the lawful first ignition of attention around an unfinished thing. The failure begins when the glow is reported as proof that the thing already has mass.

## Distinction from Artificial Inanity

[[Artificial Inanity]] names output that has the form of meaningfulness without the freight. Pitch Glow names an evaluation condition: the surrounding honor-system temporarily amplifies a thing's apparent meaningfulness.

An AI-generated campaign bible can be Artificial Inanity. A human-made campaign bible can be trapped in Pitch Glow. The two often cooperate, because polished empty output is unusually good at producing glow in rooms that reward fluency.

## Significance for Interdimensional Semiotics

Pitch Glow contributes to [[Narrative Drift Models]] by naming a drift engine that operates before a narrative enters durable use. A symbol may drift at the moment of its public birth because the presentation layer teaches the cohort to honor the wrong object: the feeling of potential rather than the structure that can carry potential into substrate.

The diagnostic test is IntSem's second principle: subtract the honoring and ask what remains.
