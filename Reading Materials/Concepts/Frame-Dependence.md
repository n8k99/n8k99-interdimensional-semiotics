# Frame-Dependence

**Frame-Dependence** names the property of a quantity, truth, or identity whose value is fixed *only relative to a frame of reference* and changes when the frame changes — as against a **frame-independent** (invariant, absolute) quantity, which holds the same value under every admissible frame. The distinction is an *axis*, not a verdict: the interesting question about any object is never "is it true?" but "is its truth-value invariant under change of frame, or does it ride the frame?" Position, simultaneity, velocity, and "left vs. right" are the textbook frame-dependent quantities; the speed of light, the spacetime interval, and a [[Topological Invariant|topological invariant]] are the textbook frame-independent ones. In the field, frame-dependence is the formal handle on the whole problem IntSem exists to study — *the same signifier indexing a different object depending on who, when, and from where it is read* — and it is the load-bearing distinction-axis the [[Library of Libraries]] theory turns on.

## Cross-Book Development

The concept arrives in IntSem from three directions that converge on the same axis.

**The physics inheritance.** In relativity, a frame of reference is a coordinate system attached to an observer, and the central discovery is *which* quantities survive the change of observer. Length and duration do not (they contract and dilate — frame-dependent); the spacetime interval does (Lorentz-invariant — frame-independent). The lesson IntSem extracts is not the physics but the *form*: any rigorous account of a domain must sort its quantities into the invariant ring and the frame-dependent functions, because confusing the two is the characteristic error. (Compare [[Temporal Drift]], where "mass" is explicitly flagged as frame-dependent in relativity and a scalar in Newton — the same name indexing different mathematical objects across regimes.)

**The semiotic inheritance.** [[Map Is Not the Territory|The map is not the territory]]: a representation is always a representation *from somewhere*, and the "somewhere" is a frame. [[Incommensurability]] is what frame-dependence looks like when two frames cannot be inter-translated — the paradigms do not merely disagree, they price the same terms in different and unconvertible coordinates. Frame-dependence is therefore the genus; incommensurability is the severe species where no frame-change map exists.

**The "located not composed" inheritance.** The sharpest statement comes from the [[Library of Libraries]] theory and its philosophical seed. [[Borges]]' *Library of Babel* holds every book that *could be imagined* — but those books are **located, not composed**: they pre-exist any author as addresses in an orderless substrate, and a mind does not *write* one, it *finds* one. Finding is frame-relative — *which* book you locate, *where* it sits, *what* it neighbors, depends entirely on the lens you bring. Against this stands the [[Frege]]/[[Whitehead]] pole: timeless thoughts and eternal objects, books *entailed* by the structure of reality itself — "not authored, not imagined, but cast, the way a shadow is cast." Those are frame-*independent*: true (or entailed) whether or not anyone, anywhere, imagines them. The whole architecture hangs on this single axis.

## Significance for Interdimensional Semiotics

Frame-dependence is the axis along which IntSem's two foundational locations are split, and the reason the split is *structural* rather than decorative:

- **[[All Possible Books]]** (the outer wing) = the **frame-dependent** pole. Borges' substrate, *located not composed*. Total and orderless; meaning is made, not given; the same Babel page is catalogued differently in different ages, by different lenses, by civilisations who find opposite meanings in the same noise. Content given; ordering authored.
- **[[All Potential Books]]** (the inner room) = the **frame-independent** pole. Frege's timeless thoughts and Whitehead's eternal objects. Books *entailed*, true in every frame, the fixed point from which the whole Library hangs.

The [[2026-06-12|June 11 thread]] worked this into a precise claim. An atomic note has **one content coordinate** — its address in the orderless Babel substrate, *invariant*: the seed sits at the same address no matter who looks — and **N hierarchy coordinates** (Historical, Temporal, Spatial, Organizational, Epic), each *frame-dependent*: change the lens, change the position, change the neighbors. "A lens is a frame." So the [[Library of Libraries]] is, in-world, frame-dependence *worked*: content coordinate invariant, lens position frame-dependent. This is the doctrine the lore note names directly — *"content coordinate invariant, lens position frame-dependent."*

Two deeper turns from the same thread are load-bearing for the field and worth recording precisely:

**Frame-dependence, serialized, is invariant.** The catalogue is pure frame-dependent structure — it *is* the made orderings, the five hierarchies. Yet as a π-segment it has an invariant content coordinate. So the most frame-dependent object in the system casts an invariant shadow: a frame is *"a function whose graph is a fixed point in the substrate it filters."* And one floor higher: the cosmos itself is a frame — its position fixed by a [[CIF (Cosmic Index Factor)|CIF]]-address — π↔Oᵢ is the largest frame-transformation the system admits (changing every address, every neighbor, every digit), and the **invariant of invariants** that survives it is exactly the [[Kleene's recursion theorem|self-locating fixed point]] — defined by *structure* (frame-free), not by *digits* (frame-bound). This is where frame-dependence connects to the **measure-zero cross-cosmos door**: causal leakage between cosmi (π ↔ Oᵢ) is total-and-therefore-inert in the bulk, and happens *only* on a shared fixed point — the one address that does not have to be *found* (located, frame-relative) because it *reconstructs itself from its own structure* (frame-independent). Frames touch only at the invariant.

**The §V frontier — the part with no invariant residue.** The thread then *corrected itself*. The tidy picture (content invariant, lens frame-dependent, everything casts an invariant shadow) is not the whole truth. Consider the musician who does not carry a song into the room but *finds the part that fits* what is already playing: that note "has no value until the other instruments define the frame, and then it's fully determined, and then it's gone. Pure frame-dependent quantity with no invariant residue." This is frame-dependence in its *true* (strongest) form — not "an invariant content seen through a lens" but **a value that comes into being only relationally, at the moment of contact, and would be a different value in a different room.** It is why the interpretive work of the inner room "must be done by a person (or a ghost), never a query" — a query *retrieves* an invariant; a player *fits* a frame-dependent part. This is the live edge of the concept, not a settled definition.
