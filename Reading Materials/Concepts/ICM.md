# ICM (Idealized Cognitive Model)

An Idealized Cognitive Model is a structured mental model that organizes knowledge and gives rise to prototype effects when it fails to perfectly match the world. As introduced by [[Lakoff - Women Fire and Dangerous Things|Lakoff]] in *Women, Fire, and Dangerous Things* (ch. 4), ICMs are the cognitive structures that determine how humans categorize experience. They come in four types: propositional models (frames and schemas specifying entities, properties, and relations), image-schematic models (kinesthetic patterns like containment, path, and force), metaphoric models (systematic cross-domain mappings), and metonymic models (part-for-whole and exemplar-for-category relations). When an ICM fits the world neatly, categorization appears classical; when the fit is imperfect, prototype effects emerge.

The ICM concept resonates with structures described at other scales by other authors on the reading list. [[Kuhn - Structure of Scientific Revolutions|Kuhn's]] paradigm (in its broader sense of "disciplinary matrix") functions as a community-level ICM: it organizes what counts as a problem, what counts as a solution, and what counts as a relevant similarity. [[Barthes - Mythologies|Barthes']] mythological systems operate similarly at the cultural level, naturalizing contingent idealized models until they appear to be simple descriptions of reality. [[Foucault - Order of Things|Foucault's]] episteme extends the logic to civilizational scale: entire epochs share deep structural assumptions about how knowledge is organized that function as ICMs writ large.

Together these suggest a fractal structure of meaning-organization: ICMs at the individual-cognitive level, paradigms at the community level, myths at the cultural level, epistemes at the civilizational level — each a structured model that determines what can be thought within its domain and produces "prototype effects" (anomalies, marginal cases, contested membership) at its boundaries.

## Cross-Book Development

[[Lakoff - Women Fire and Dangerous Things|Lakoff's]] treatment of ICMs in chapter 4 of *Women, Fire, and Dangerous Things* is the theoretical centerpiece of his non-classical theory of categorization. The key insight is that prototype effects are not directly part of category structure but are by-products of the interaction between idealized models and a world that does not perfectly conform to them. The category "bachelor" has a clear ICM (unmarried adult male), but the ICM assumes a cultural context in which marriage is the norm — the Pope, Tarzan, and a man in a long-term partnership all fail to fit the ICM cleanly, producing graded judgments of "bachelorhood." This means prototype effects are evidence about cognitive models, not about categories themselves.

The four types of ICM map onto the major theoretical resources of the reading list. Propositional ICMs correspond to the frame semantics tradition; image-schematic ICMs ground categorization in the embodied patterns described in [[Lakoff Johnson - Metaphors We Live By|Lakoff and Johnson's]] treatment of orientational and ontological metaphor (ch. 4, 6); metaphoric ICMs are the systematic cross-domain mappings that constitute conceptual metaphor itself; and metonymic ICMs capture the operations Lakoff analyzes on pp. 77-79 and 107-8, where a part or salient member stands for the whole category.

[[Kuhn - Structure of Scientific Revolutions|Kuhn's]] parallel is particularly instructive. A scientific paradigm functions as an ICM for a research community: it specifies what entities exist, what questions are meaningful, and what counts as a solution. When the paradigm fits the phenomena well, science proceeds "normally." When anomalies accumulate — when the ICM fails to match the world — crisis results, producing the paradigm-shift analog of Lakoff's prototype effects. [[Kurzweil - How to Create a Mind|Kurzweil's]] hierarchical model can be read as describing the neural implementation of ICMs: each level of the neocortical hierarchy maintains a predictive model of its input, and when the model fails to predict (when the ICM mismatches), a learning signal propagates upward to revise the model.

## Significance for Interdimensional Semiotics

ICMs are central to [[Interdimensional Semiotics]] because they are the units of meaning-organization most likely to vary across dimensions. Different embodiments produce different image-schematic ICMs; different cultures produce different propositional ICMs; different physics produce different metaphoric ICMs. The IS question is whether there exists a level of ICM structure that is invariant across all these variations — a "deep ICM" that constrains what cognitive models are possible for any embodied intelligence in any physical reality.

The fractal structure of ICMs (individual to civilizational) also connects to the IS concept of semiotic gravitational mass. An ICM that operates at multiple scales simultaneously — grounded in image schemas, reinforced by cultural myths, embedded in civilizational epistemes — has enormous semiotic mass and will resist narrative drift even under extreme translational pressure. The [[connectomes]] in [[The Noosphere]] can be understood as carriers of ICM structure: what gets transmitted across substrates is not raw data but the structured models through which data becomes meaningful. Whether those models survive transit depends on whether the receiving substrate can generate the embodied and cultural conditions necessary to support them.
