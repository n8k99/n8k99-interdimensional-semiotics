# Image Schema

An image schema is a recurrent, pre-conceptual pattern arising from bodily experience — containment, path, force, balance, center-periphery — that provides the skeletal structure for abstract reasoning and metaphorical mapping. First systematized by [[Lakoff Johnson - Metaphors We Live By|Lakoff and Johnson]] and developed extensively in [[Lakoff - Women Fire and Dangerous Things|Lakoff's]] *Women, Fire, and Dangerous Things* (pp. 105-8, 267-75), image schemas are not rich mental images but stripped-down dynamic patterns: the experience of moving along a path, being inside a container, encountering a barrier. They occupy the critical middle position in the grounding chain between raw sensorimotor experience and fully articulated conceptual metaphor.

Image schemas develop across the reading list as the mechanism that connects embodied experience to abstract thought. [[Lakoff Johnson - Metaphors We Live By|Lakoff and Johnson]] argue in the Afterword (line 7482) that image schemas underlie primary metaphors — the universal mappings acquired in infancy when source and target experiences are still fused. [[Hofstadter - Godel Escher Bach|Hofstadter's]] concept of isomorphism can be read as a formal account of what image schemas accomplish: they are the structural invariants preserved across mappings between experiential domains and conceptual domains. [[Kurzweil - How to Create a Mind|Kurzweil's]] hierarchical pattern recognition model suggests a neural substrate: the lowest levels of the neocortical hierarchy encode precisely the kind of recurrent spatial and dynamic patterns that image schemas describe.

The concept is significant because it identifies the most primitive layer of meaning — the level at which cognition is most directly shaped by physics. If meaning is built upward from image schemas, then the character of a cognitive system's meaning-world depends on what schemas its body can generate.

## Cross-Book Development

[[Lakoff Johnson - Metaphors We Live By|Lakoff and Johnson]] introduce image schemas implicitly through their analyses of orientational metaphor (ch. 4: HAPPY IS UP, SAD IS DOWN) and ontological metaphor (ch. 6: MIND IS A CONTAINER), though the term is not fully theorized until the Afterword and in Lakoff's subsequent work. The orientational metaphors depend on the UP-DOWN schema derived from the body's experience of gravity; the container metaphors depend on the IN-OUT schema derived from the body's experience of bounded space. These are not arbitrary associations but structural necessities of being an upright, bounded organism in a gravitational field.

[[Lakoff - Women Fire and Dangerous Things|Lakoff]] provides the full treatment in *Women, Fire, and Dangerous Things*, where image schemas serve as one of four types of cognitive model within the ICM framework (ch. 4). Image-schematic models — kinesthetic patterns abstracted from sensorimotor experience — ground the more complex propositional, metaphoric, and metonymic models. The CONTAINER schema, for instance, underlies the propositional logic of classical categorization (entities are either IN or OUT of a category), while the PATH schema structures narrative, argumentation, and temporal reasoning. Lakoff's account (pp. 267-75) makes clear that image schemas are not static structures but dynamic patterns with internal logic: a PATH has a source, a trajectory, and a goal; a FORCE has a direction, magnitude, and point of application.

[[Kurzweil - How to Create a Mind|Kurzweil's]] Pattern Recognition Theory of Mind offers a computational implementation. The approximately 300 million neocortical pattern-recognition modules are organized hierarchically, with lower levels detecting exactly the kinds of spatial, temporal, and force-dynamic regularities that constitute image schemas. [[Hofstadter - Godel Escher Bach|Hofstadter's]] treatment of recursion and levels of description complements this: image schemas are the base case of a recursive process that builds from sensorimotor pattern to metaphor to theoretical framework, each level operating on the output of the level below.

## Significance for Interdimensional Semiotics

Image schemas represent the most substrate-dependent layer of the semiotic stack and therefore pose the sharpest test case for [[Interdimensional Semiotics]]. If a dimension operates under different physical laws — no gravity, no bounded objects, no force interactions — then the UP-DOWN, CONTAINER, and FORCE schemas would not arise from embodied experience. The entire edifice of conceptual metaphor built on those schemas would lack grounding. This suggests that symbolic persistence across dimensions cannot be taken for granted at the schematic level; it must be achieved, if at all, at higher levels of abstraction where the dependence on specific image schemas has been attenuated.

Yet image schemas may also be the most likely candidates for cross-dimensional universals, precisely because they are so primitive. Any embodied intelligence navigating a spatial environment with constraints will develop some version of PATH, BOUNDARY, and FORCE — the specific physics differ, but the schematic structure may converge. This hypothesis connects to the IS concept of semiotic gravitational mass: schemas grounded in very general physical constraints (topology, not specific forces) may have greater mass and resist narrative drift more effectively than schemas tied to particular physical constants. The [[connectomes]] in [[The Noosphere]] that encode image-schematic structure may be among the most durable carriers of cross-dimensional meaning.
