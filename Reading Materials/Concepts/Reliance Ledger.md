# Reliance Ledger

A **Reliance Ledger** is the portion of a persistence layer that records which honored meanings have induced action, cost, exposure, or obligation, so later amendment cannot masquerade as clean erasure.

The term extends [[Semiotic Estoppel]]. Semiotic Estoppel names the binding residue created by reliance. Reliance Ledger names the memory-practice that preserves evidence of that reliance inside a meaning-system.

## Definition

A reliance ledger tracks:

- the honored statement, rule, promise, identity, or institutional position;
- the agents or cohorts who changed position under it;
- the costs, foreclosed alternatives, permissions, disclosures, or obligations created by that change;
- the later denials, amendments, or reinterpretations that would need to account for the reliance.

The ledger need not be literally financial. It is a legal-semiotic accounting structure.

## Distinction From Persistence Layer

A [[Persistence Layer]] remembers what a system has said or done. A Reliance Ledger remembers what others were induced to do because the system said or did it.

This distinction matters because a system can preserve its own continuity while losing the history of dependence it created outside itself. Such memory is useful for brand consistency but inadequate for ethics.

## Significance for Interdimensional Semiotics

Reliance Ledger contributes to [[Symbolic Persistence Theory]] by naming a maintenance practice for symbols whose survival depends on obligation rather than present belief. It constrains amendment, rebranding, canon revision, constitutional rewriting, and AI-memory pruning by asking what reliance would be injured by the change.
