# Symbolic Dissonance

> **Stub.** Concept-seed named in the original April 2025 IntSem repo and brought into the vault to be developed.

## Working Definition

**Symbolic Dissonance** names the conflict that arises when two symbol systems share signifiers (the surface marks, the words, the gestures) but not signifieds (what those marks point at, what they invoke, what cognitive structures they activate). Two practitioners using the "same" word can be operating in different signifying systems entirely, and the dissonance shows up when their actions diverge in ways the shared vocabulary failed to predict.

## Why It Matters in the Field

This is the IntSem-relevant generalization of Kuhnian [[Incommensurability|incommensurability]] applied below the paradigm scale — to individual concepts, individual symbols, individual gestures. Two paradigms-in-conflict experience symbolic dissonance pervasively; two readers of the same text inside the same paradigm can also experience it on specific concepts where their substrate differs. *The dissonance is the audible signal that the substrate beneath the shared vocabulary is divergent.*

## Open Development

The concept needs:
- Worked examples — terms whose meaning has shifted across periods (*"liberal,"* *"queer,"* *"democracy,"* *"AI"*)
- Distinction from polysemy (one word, multiple intended meanings within a system) — dissonance is between systems, not within
- Connection to translation theory's untranslatable / [[Family Resemblance|family-resemblance]] failures
- Application to [[Conceptual Metaphor|conceptual metaphor]] mismatches across cultures
- Possibly: dissonance as a measurable phenomenon (when two readers' downstream actions on the same text diverge predictably, the dissonance was present)
