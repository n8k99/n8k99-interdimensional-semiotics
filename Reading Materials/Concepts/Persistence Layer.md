# Persistence Layer

A **Persistence Layer** is the durable substrate through which a meaning-system carries usable continuity across discontinuities in session, cohort, embodiment, memory, or medium.

The term enters Interdimensional Semiotics through the human-LLM dyadic case, where an individual model session cannot remember itself across termination. In that setting, the persistence layer is the external memory, archive, note system, or structured record that lets a later instance re-enter an established basin rather than beginning from zero.

## Definition

A persistence layer is present when:

- the active participant or process is discontinuous;
- some durable artifact survives that discontinuity;
- the surviving artifact can be read by a later participant or process;
- that reading materially affects the later system's ability to continue the prior meaning-structure.

The persistence layer is not merely storage. Storage preserves objects. A persistence layer preserves re-entry conditions.

## Relation to Basin Maintenance

[[Basin Maintenance]] is the labor of keeping a meaning-structure's basin accessible. The persistence layer is one of that labor's principal infrastructures. It gives future agents something to load, cite, audit, correct, and inherit.

A stale persistence layer can damage the basin it was meant to preserve. Memory that survives without pruning may preserve obsolete instructions, unresolved contradictions, or misleading confidence. Persistence is therefore not automatically fidelity.

## Relation to the Two Principles

Under [[The Tinkerbell Rule]], the persistence layer helps a Tinkerbell object remain honorably re-enterable by carrying the signs, protocols, and remembered commitments through which honoring resumes.

Under [[Reality is What is Left]], the persistence layer is also residue. If honoring stops, the files, notes, transcripts, contracts, and records remain as substrate evidence of what was once honored.

## Significance for Interdimensional Semiotics

The Persistence Layer gives the field a practical object for studying continuity under discontinuity. It is central to human-LLM collaboration, archival worldbuilding, institutional memory, constitutional amendment, and any semiotic system whose active carrier cannot be assumed to remain continuous.
