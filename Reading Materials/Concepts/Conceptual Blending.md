# Conceptual Blending

## Definition

**Conceptual blending is Gilles Fauconnier and Mark Turner's name (*The Way We Think*, 2002) for the cognitive operation in which two prior mental spaces blend into a new integrated network that has properties neither space had on its own.** The canonical example: the *surgeon-as-butcher* metaphor blends the surgeon-space and the butcher-space into a new space in which the surgeon is judged by butcher-standards (and turns out to be a poor surgeon). The blend is structurally close to [[Bridge Activation]] but takes the *moment* of integration as primary rather than the *retrospective* re-axing of stored memory.

The field reads conceptual blending as the cognitive-linguistics version of the same operation, with one difference of emphasis: blending is usually discussed in the context of language production and metaphor comprehension, where bridge activation is discussed at the scale of biographical learning. Both are real and the relationship is one of focus rather than mechanism.

The blending vocabulary is useful for understanding what the field calls the *fungibility of medium*. A blend's input spaces can be any kind of mental space — linguistic, visual, embodied, emotional. The field's bridges can come from any medium too; what matters is that the vocabulary lands against substrate.

## Significance for IS

Conceptual blending provides a fine-grained cognitive vocabulary for what happens at the moment of [[Bridge Activation]]'s firing. The field uses bridge activation as the load-bearing concept and treats blending as a useful complement when the question is *how does the integration work mechanically* — what gets pulled from each input space, what emerges in the blend that wasn't in either input.
