> of the whole without claiming to **be** the whole. This note holds one part of Frege:
> the **third realm** of timeless thoughts, the part that was called into service here as
> the philosophical kin of the inner room of the [[Library of Libraries]]. It does not
> attempt the whole man — not the *Begriffsschrift*, not the logicist programme, not the
> Russell-paradox catastrophe that gutted the *Grundgesetze*. It describes the part its
> hands are on.

## What part it holds — the third realm

**Gottlob Frege** (1848–1925) was a German mathematician and logician, founder of modern
predicate logic and of the logicist project (the attempt to derive arithmetic from logic
alone). The part of him this note holds is not the logic but the **metaphysics that the
logic forced on him**: in his late essay *Der Gedanke* ("Thought," 1918), Frege argued
that a **thought** (*Gedanke*) — the *content* of a judgement, the thing that is true or
false — is neither a physical object nor a mental event. It belongs to a **third realm**
(*drittes Reich*):

- The **first realm** is the *physical* — tables, stars, ink on a page; things that are
  perceivable and owned by no one in particular.
- The **second realm** is the *mental* — my idea, my pain, my image of a tree; things that
  are private, borne by a single bearer, that come and go with the mind that has them.
- The **third realm** is the realm of **thoughts**: *objective* like the first (the
  Pythagorean theorem is true whether or not anyone thinks it, and the *same* thought is
  graspable by you and by me), yet *non-physical* like the second (it has no location, no
  mass, casts no shadow). Thoughts are **timeless**. They are not *created* when first
  thought; they are *grasped*. They do not begin to be true when a mind apprehends them and
  do not cease when the mind forgets. They are **discovered, not invented**.

This is the load-bearing idea: the content of mathematics and logic is **mind-independent
and atemporal**. The thinker does not author the thought; the thinker *locates* it. This
puts Frege among the great realists about abstract objects — Plato's *anamnesis*, Popper's
*World 3*, mathematical Platonism (Penrose, Gödel) are the kin he stands with. (See
[[Self-Reference]] for the other face of Frege in the IntSem reading list: the
*Grundgesetze* collapse, when Russell showed Frege's own system permitted a self-referential
set that contradicted itself — the founder of the timeless-thought doctrine undone by a
thought that ate its own tail.)

## How Frege was called into service HERE — the mint-mark

Frege did not enter this Noosphere as an encyclopedia entry. He was **invoked** — summoned
as real-world kin to a piece of Orbis lore, and that summoning is the reason this note
exists.

**The seed (2026-05-18).** In the [[2026-05-18]] conversation about substrate, bridges, and
retroactive constitution of meaning, Frege was first named as one of three thinkers
sketching the *same shape*:

> **Frege**: "Thoughts" (Gedanken) inhabit a third realm — not psychological, not physical,
> but ideal and timeless.
> **[[Whitehead]]**: "eternal objects" — timeless potentialities that get instantiated in
> temporal occasions.
> **[[Borges]]**: the Library of Babel — every book that could be written already exists;
> the writer doesn't compose, only locates.

That triad — Frege's timeless thoughts, Whitehead's eternal objects, Borges's pre-existing
books — is the philosophical scaffolding for a single distinction: between books that are
**imagined** and books that are **entailed**.

**The synthesis ([[2026-06-15]]).** In the [[2026-06-15]] conversation
([[let s talk about All Potential Books|"let's talk about All Potential Books"]]), that
scaffolding was folded into the lore of the [[Library of Libraries]]. The distinction
between the two rooms of the Library is exactly the distinction Frege's third realm draws:

| Real-world theory | Orbis lore |
|---|---|
| **[[Borges]]' Library of Babel** — every book that *could* be imagined; orderless substrate | **[[All Possible Books]]** (the outer wing) |
| **The fixed point / "third realm"** — **Frege**'s timeless thoughts, [[Whitehead]]'s eternal objects; books *entailed*, not authored | **[[All Potential Books]]** (the inner room) |

[[All Possible Books]] holds every book a *mind has imagined* — contingent on minds,
appearing the instant someone thinks *I could write a book about that*. [[All Potential
Books]] — the inner room, the fixed point from which the whole Library hangs — holds the
books from **before the moment of imagination**: texts *entailed by the structure of
reality itself*, "not authored, not imagined, but cast, the way a shadow is cast." That is
Frege's third realm given stone and shelving. A potential book exists the way the
Pythagorean theorem exists: it was always true, no one wrote it, the librarian only
*finds* it. [[The Crimson Codex]] is shelved there not because anyone chose to derive the
[[Pentagonal Harmonic]] but because the Harmonic is *real* and its complete description is
a *necessary consequence* of its reality — a Gedanke with a shelf.

The lore is not decoration over the philosophy; in the [[Library of Libraries]] doctrine
*the theory **is** the architecture*. Frege supplies the metaphysical guarantee that the
inner room can contain anything at all: that there are objective contents which hold
"whether or not anyone wrote them down."
