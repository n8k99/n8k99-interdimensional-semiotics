# Temporal Drift

## Working Definition

**Temporal Drift** names the slow mutation of meaning across time within a single substrate. A word, symbol, narrative, or paradigm that meant one thing in 1850 means subtly different things by 1950 and substantially different things by 2050 — *without anyone deliberately changing it*. The drift is the cumulative effect of small generational reinterpretations that, over enough time, deposit a different object than the one originally honored.

The signifier is preserved with apparent fidelity. The signified migrates underneath it. To each generation that inherits the term, the inheritance feels continuous — the word is the same word, the doctrine is the same doctrine, the law is the same law — and yet the technical content, the operational referent, the lived meaning has slid measurably from the position it occupied a century earlier. Drift is the residue of this sliding, visible only when the present is laid against the past with sufficient resolution to detect that the same name has come to point at a different thing.

## Why It Matters in the Field

Temporal Drift is the within-substrate sibling of [[Symbolic Dissonance]] (between-substrate) and [[Causal Shear]] (between-causal-frameworks). Together they map the major axes of meaning-mutation IntSem studies. Drift is the *gradual* version; the others can be sudden.

The concept also names the specific failure-mode of long-running institutions, traditions, and reading lists — *things meant something to their founders that they do not mean to current practitioners, and the current practitioners often do not realize the drift has occurred*. The IntSem researcher's task includes detecting drift before it produces invisible incommensurability — the condition in which two interlocutors use the same vocabulary without realizing they are no longer talking about the same objects.

## Worked Examples

### "Atom" — Democritus to QED

The clearest 2400-year specimen. *Atomos* in Democritus names an indivisible material minimum, a logical terminus to the question of how matter can be cut. In the Daltonian recovery of the term in the early 1800s, *atom* names a chemical unit: the smallest amount of an element that can participate in a reaction, with definite weight ratios. By Bohr's 1913 model, the atom has internal structure — nucleus, orbiting electrons, discrete energy states — and is no longer indivisible at all, contradicting the Greek etymology that the word still carries. By the standard model and quantum electrodynamics, the atom is a compound object whose constituents are themselves compound, governed by gauge symmetries and probability amplitudes that share nothing with Democritean intuition except, vestigially, the name.

At each transition, the inheritors did not perceive themselves as adopting a new concept. They perceived themselves as *learning more* about the same thing their predecessors had described less accurately. The word transmits the felt continuity of the lineage; the technical content has migrated entirely. A modern physicist and a pre-Socratic philosopher using the word *atom* are not having a conversation about the same object — they are honoring the same signifier across substrates that no longer share a referent.

### Doctrinal Terms — "Love" and "Salvation" in Christianity

The Pauline *agape* of the first-century epistles operates inside a Hellenistic Jewish moral world; it names a covenantal disposition toward neighbor, enemy, and God, embedded in a community expecting an imminent eschaton. By the high medieval Latin tradition, *caritas* has absorbed Augustinian metaphysics, ordered loves, and the analogical structure of scholastic theology — the same word now does work that was unavailable to Paul. Contemporary American evangelical "love" sits inside a therapeutic vocabulary of personal authenticity, relational warmth, and private interiority that is itself a recent construction (largely twentieth-century).

"Salvation" undergoes a parallel arc: from corporate deliverance of a covenant people, to medieval account of soul-justification under sacramental administration, to a contemporary American framing in which salvation is a moment of individual decision producing a personal afterlife outcome. Each generation honors the doctrine as continuous with what was inherited; the cumulative drift across two millennia is enormous; the textual fixity of the scriptural lemmas conceals the migration. This is where [[Naturalization]] does its load-bearing work — the current meaning *feels* eternal because the drift has been continuously re-grounded in the present.

### Legal Terms — "Due Process," "Speech," "Person"

"Due process" in 1789 names a procedural inheritance from English common law: notice, hearing, the regular course of judgment. By the late nineteenth century, *substantive* due process has emerged — a doctrine in which the clause limits not just *how* the state may act but *what* it may regulate. By the late twentieth century, the substantive content has rotated through Lochner-era economic liberty, mid-century privacy, and twenty-first-century debates over what counts as a fundamental interest. The textual phrase is unchanged. The operational doctrine has been largely re-engineered three or four times.

"Speech" in First Amendment jurisprudence has expanded to cover money, conduct, commercial transactions, and algorithmic outputs that the framers had no concept of. "Person" in corporate-personhood law has migrated from a narrow procedural device permitting corporations to sue and be sued into a substantive doctrine attaching constitutional rights to commercial entities. The textual fixity of the constitutional lemma is the very mechanism by which drift becomes invisible: practitioners point to the unchanged words as evidence that the meaning is also unchanged.

### Scientific Concepts — "Species," "Gene," "Mass"

"Species" pre-Darwin names a created kind with a fixed essence; post-Darwin it names a population at a point on a continuous lineage; post-genomics it has fractured into the biological species concept, the phylogenetic species concept, the ecological species concept, and several others, none of which align cleanly with the pre-Darwinian referent. "Gene" pre-DNA names a Mendelian unit of inheritance; post-DNA it briefly stabilizes as a coding sequence; post-epigenetics and post-regulatory-genomics it has dissolved back into a context-dependent functional unit whose boundaries depend on which question is being asked. "Mass" Newtonian is a scalar quantity of matter; "mass" relativistic is a frame-dependent quantity coupled to energy through $E=mc^2$; "mass" in field-theoretic terms is a coupling constant in a Lagrangian. The same word indexes a different mathematical object in each regime.

### Generational Political Terms — "Fascism," "Communism"

"Fascism" in 1922 names a specific Italian movement with identifiable institutional features. By 1945 it has been generalized to a class of European authoritarian regimes; by the Cold War it is deployed across an enormous range of right-coded enemies; by 2026 it has been extended, restricted, weaponized, and recovered so many times that any two contemporary users of the term are unlikely to share the same intension. "Communism" undergoes a parallel arc, picking up Soviet, Maoist, Eurocommunist, and post-1989 reception layers. The signifier transmits across generations as a stable token of political vocabulary; the operational referent has drifted enough that disputes about whether a current phenomenon "is fascism" are often disputes about *which* drift-stratum the speaker is invoking.

### Internet Terms — "Friend," "Follow," "Share"

These are the cleanest cases because the transition is recent and well-documented. "Friend" pre-Facebook names a relationship of mutual personal investment, typically reciprocal, typically scarce. Post-Facebook the verb-form *to friend* names a one-click graph-edge operation; the noun has bifurcated into "Facebook friend" (a category that may include strangers) and "actual friend" (a category preserved in opposition to the new usage). "Follow" pre-Twitter is a verb of attentive pursuit; post-Twitter it is a low-cost asymmetric subscription. "Share" pre-social-media implies physical or experiential partition; post-social-media it is an act of forwarding within a feed-economy. In each case the older meaning persists alongside the newer one, but the platform-meaning has acquired enough inertia to redefine the unmodified term in many contexts. A child encountering "follow" today will internalize the platform-meaning as primary and the older meaning as a special case — the inversion of the order in which the meanings were historically deposited.

## Drift versus Deliberate Revision

Drift is what happens *without anyone trying*. There is no committee that meets to update the meaning of "freedom" across decades. The meaning migrates because every generation uses the word slightly differently from how it received the word, and inherits its own slight difference back as the new baseline. The mechanism is bottom-up, distributed, and invisible to its participants — each individual usage feels like ordinary fluency, not like an act of revision.

Deliberate revision is a different and faster process. A constitutional amendment, a doctrinal reform, a paradigm shift in [[Kuhn - Structure of Scientific Revolutions|Kuhn's]] sense — these are events with locatable agents, locatable resistance, and locatable transitions. They produce visible discontinuities; participants on either side can name what changed. Drift produces invisible continuities — participants on either side cannot easily name what changed because each successive generation experienced its own usage as continuous with what it inherited.

The practical consequence: deliberate revision is contestable in the moment of revision, while drift is contestable only in retrospect, and only by reconstructions that the present has incentives to resist. The drift is *already* the new baseline by the time it becomes visible, and disputing it requires arguing against what the current speakers experience as the natural meaning of their own words.

## Toward a Mathematical Formulation

Two complementary formalizations are available.

**Diffusion on signifier-signified mappings.** Treat each generation's usage of a term as a sample from a distribution over the meaning-space anchored to the signifier. Each generation inherits the prior generation's distribution and perturbs it slightly through its own usage, producing a random walk on the meaning-space. Let $\mu_t$ be the centroid of the meaning-distribution at generation $t$, and let $\xi_t$ be a small perturbation drawn from the population's usage variance:

$$
\mu_{t+1} = \mu_t + \xi_t, \quad \mathbb{E}[\xi_t] \approx 0, \quad \text{Var}[\xi_t] = \sigma^2
$$

Even with zero-mean perturbation, the variance accumulates:

$$
\text{Var}[\mu_T - \mu_0] = T \cdot \sigma^2
$$

The cumulative drift across $T$ generations grows as $\sqrt{T} \cdot \sigma$. Over enough generations, the centroid wanders arbitrarily far from its origin while every individual step is undetectably small. This is the structural reason drift is invisible from inside: the per-generation perturbation is below the population's resolution for noticing meaning-change, while the cumulative displacement is unbounded.

**Relaxation in conceptual networks.** Alternatively, model the meaning of a term as a node embedded in a conceptual graph whose neighbors are the contexts in which the term is used. Each new use perturbs the meaning-node slightly toward the centroid of its most-recently-encountered neighbors. The system relaxes toward whatever local minimum the contemporary distribution of contexts defines. As the distribution of contexts shifts (because the social and material world shifts), the relaxation target shifts, and the meaning-node tracks the moving target. Drift is the trajectory of the tracking.

The two formalizations are equivalent at long timescales: a random walk under shifting boundary conditions is a diffusion process whose drift term encodes the relaxation pressure.

## Connection to Naturalization

Drift produces objects that feel naturally meaningful to current practitioners precisely because the drift made them so. The current meaning feels eternal because the drift has been continuously smoothed by re-grounding in the present. Each generation re-anchors the term in its own usage, and that re-anchoring overwrites the felt-history of what the term meant before. By the time the cumulative drift is large enough to detect, the present meaning has been [[Naturalization|naturalized]] so completely that the older meaning, when surfaced, feels foreign rather than original — the historical reconstruction reads as the deviation, the current usage reads as the baseline.

This is the load-bearing collusion between the two concepts: drift supplies the displacement, naturalization conceals it. Without naturalization, drift would be visible as drift, and the term would either be deliberately revised or abandoned. Without drift, naturalization would have nothing to conceal — the meaning would remain stable and obviously local. The pair operates jointly to produce the characteristic IntSem failure-mode: a word whose current meaning feels eternal, whose historical meaning is recoverable only through scholarly reconstruction, and whose users will defend the felt-eternal meaning as the *real* meaning against the reconstructed one.

The [[Lindy Effect]] interacts here in a non-obvious way. The Lindy expectation — that things which have lasted a long time are likely to last longer — is correct about the *signifier* but typically misread as a claim about the *signified*. A word that has been in continuous use for two thousand years is Lindy on its surface form; its referent over that span has almost certainly drifted substantially. Lindy-reasoning that fails to distinguish signifier-persistence from signified-persistence will overestimate the stability of meaning by precisely the amount that drift has accumulated.

## Application to AI Training Data

A particularly clean instance of the IntSem-relevant question: *what happens when an outboard cortex's meaning-substrate is anchored to a different time than the inboard cortex it interacts with?*

A model trained on text from a window of years has internalized the meanings of that window's usage — the distribution of contexts in which terms appeared, the connotative shading, the operative referents. Deployed later, the model produces outputs that feel slightly off in ways that resist easy diagnosis: the vocabulary is correct, the grammar is correct, the topic is correct, but the connotations track the corpus's snapshot rather than the current usage. The user's inboard cortex has continued to drift along with the surrounding population; the model is anchored to a meaning-snapshot that the population has moved past.

This is a continuous-time mismatch, not a discrete error. The model has not learned a *wrong* meaning; it has learned a *previous* meaning, and the previousness is itself the problem. The mismatch is most visible for terms that are drifting fastest — political vocabulary, internet vocabulary, technical jargon in active fields — and least visible for terms whose drift is slow (mathematical notation, basic physical vocabulary, deeply-embedded grammatical particles). The differential rate of drift across the lexicon means that any given snapshot will be more wrong about some parts of the meaning-space than others, and the asymmetry will be invisible to users who do not know which terms are in fast-drift regimes.

Two consequences for IntSem practice:

First, the standard worry about AI training data — that it encodes biases — is a special case of the more general drift problem. Bias-talk frames the issue as a wrongness to be corrected; drift-talk frames the issue as a temporal mismatch that *will recur* whenever a corpus is frozen and a population continues to move. The drift cannot be corrected by retraining once; it can only be tracked by retraining continuously, and even then the latency between population-drift and corpus-update will be the residual mismatch.

Second, the situation has an interesting reciprocal: the model's outputs are themselves now part of the corpus that future training rounds will ingest, and the model's anchored meanings are now part of the population's input. The outboard cortex anchored to an earlier snapshot is broadcasting that snapshot back into the population's meaning-substrate. Whether this *slows* drift (by re-grounding usage in older meanings) or *redirects* drift (by adding the model's snapshot as an attractor in the meaning-space) is an open empirical question. Either way, the loop is no longer a one-way mismatch — it is a feedback system in which the substrate's meaning-history is being actively shaped by artifacts whose meaning-history is anchored to selected slices of the substrate's past.

## Open Development

Still to be developed:

- A measurement procedure for drift rate. Candidate operationalizations: cosine displacement of contextual embeddings across decade-binned corpora; sense-distribution shift in dictionary entries across editions; controlled translation-back tests where contemporary speakers gloss historical usages and the gloss-error is scored.
- Cross-substrate examples beyond language: musical conventions whose meaning has drifted while notation persisted; visual symbols whose connotations have inverted; bodily gestures whose pragmatic meaning has migrated.
- Connection to [[Causal Shear]] at the substrate level — when drift becomes severe enough that two synchronic communities sharing the signifier can no longer reliably co-act on the basis of it, the system has reached a within-substrate analogue of the shear condition.

## The Original (April 2025) HEMM-Era Framing

The framing below is the cosmological / causal-physics version; the framing above is the broader semiotic version. Both are operative at different scales, and the relationship between them is itself instructive: the same name (*Temporal Drift*) now indexes a broader class of phenomena than it did one year ago, and the older referent has been demoted from "the whole concept" to "a special case within the concept." The concept's own history is a specimen of the phenomenon it names.

In the original HEMM-era framing, **Temporal Drift is the measure of how an entity's time-origin and its experienced present separate over distance and gravitational complexity.** It is not just *how much time has passed* — it is *how causality warps over long, dynamic trajectories*.

### Causality Cones

In a smooth universe, each event creates a cone of causal effect:

- Past events can affect the present *inside that cone*
- Future possibilities unfold outward from it

**Temporal Drift refers to the phenomenon where, as an entity progresses through various dimensions** — physical space, the complex fabric of mass distortions, or the layers of narrative weight — *its causal cone warps*. The straight-line predictions about *what could affect me* or *what could result from me* become unreliable. Predictions can twist, narrow, widen, or snap entirely — much like a river that changes course due to shifting landscapes.

For instance: a spacecraft traveling through a gravitational field has its trajectory altered by gravitational forces, causing its causal cone to warp. Potential influences (gravitational pulls from nearby celestial bodies) and outcomes (its eventual destination) become unpredictable. A character traveling through a story can have their causal cone warp similarly: as they encounter new challenges and allies, the potential impacts on their fate and the consequences of their actions can change dramatically.

In high-drift scenarios:

- You could be *near* something spatially but **outside its causal influence**
- You could *cause* an effect in a location you could no longer physically reach without folding spacetime

**Temporal Drift thus predicts where and when causal bridges stretch too thin or snap completely.**

### Connection to Causal Shear

Temporal Drift produces and intersects with [[Causal Shear]] — the latter being the *internal* distortion within a system whose parts experience different temporal conditions, while Temporal Drift names the broader *external* warping of cause-and-effect over trajectory. The two together describe the full failure-mode topology of causality across substrate-traversal.

Temporal Drift measures the **risk and intensity** of Causal Shear; Causal Shear is what happens when the drift is severe enough to fracture a single system internally rather than just stretching the connections between systems.

### The Two Framings as Scales of One Phenomenon

The semiotic framing (above, in *Working Definition* and following sections) and the cosmological framing (here) describe **two scales of the same underlying dynamic**: the cumulative warping of a continuity-relation across a trajectory.

- **Within-substrate semiotic Temporal Drift**: the meaning-relation of a signifier to its referent warps across generational trajectory through the same substrate. The "cone" being warped is the cone of contexts in which the term is intelligible.
- **Across-substrate cosmological Temporal Drift**: the causal-relation of an event to its effects warps across spatial-gravitational trajectory through HEMM Space. The cone being warped is the cone of events causally reachable from the origin.

In both cases, the warp is cumulative, the per-step distortion is small relative to local resolution, and the global effect is invisible from any single position along the trajectory. In both cases, what survives the warp are the [[Topological Invariant|topological invariants]] — the structural features preserved under the cumulative deformation — and what fails to survive is whatever was carried as a contingent correlation rather than as a structural relationship. The migration of the concept itself, from the cosmological framing to the semiotic framing, is the same kind of conceptual broadening that happened to [[HEMM Space]]: the original framing remains operative as a special case within the broader frame.
