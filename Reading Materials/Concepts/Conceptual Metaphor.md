# Conceptual Metaphor

Conceptual metaphor, as formulated by [[Lakoff Johnson - Metaphors We Live By|Lakoff and Johnson]] in chapters 1-3 of *Metaphors We Live By*, is a systematic mapping from a concrete source domain to an abstract target domain that structures how humans think and reason, not merely how they speak. ARGUMENT IS WAR, TIME IS MONEY, LIFE IS A JOURNEY — these are not poetic flourishes but cognitive structures that determine what inferences are available and what aspects of experience are highlighted or hidden. The central claim is that abstract thought is fundamentally metaphorical and grounded in embodied experience.

The concept ramifies across the IS reading list in important ways. [[Lakoff - Women Fire and Dangerous Things|Lakoff]] integrates conceptual metaphor into his broader theory of categorization and cognitive models, showing that metaphoric ICMs are one of four types of idealized cognitive model structuring human categories (ch. 4). [[Kuhn - Structure of Scientific Revolutions|Kuhn]] implicitly relies on metaphorical reasoning when he argues that paradigm shifts involve seeing familiar phenomena under a new gestalt — what changes is not the data but the conceptual mapping through which data is interpreted. [[Hofstadter - Godel Escher Bach|Hofstadter]] treats analogy (a close relative of metaphor) as the core of cognition: every act of understanding involves mapping the structure of one domain onto another. [[Barthes - Mythologies|Barthes]] describes myth as a second-order semiological system in which a complete sign becomes the signifier for a new signified — a structural operation closely parallel to metaphorical projection.

Conceptual metaphor thus occupies a pivotal position in the reading list as the mechanism by which embodied, local experience generates abstract, transferable meaning. It is the engine that converts image schemas into theoretical frameworks, sensorimotor contingencies into philosophical arguments, and bodily navigation into narrative structure.

## Cross-Book Development

The grounding chain for conceptual metaphor begins with [[Lakoff Johnson - Metaphors We Live By|Lakoff and Johnson's]] account of primary metaphor in the Afterword (line 7580): children's conflated experiences of warmth-with-affection or height-with-quantity produce universal metaphorical mappings (AFFECTION IS WARMTH, MORE IS UP) that serve as the atomic building blocks of more complex metaphors. These primary metaphors draw on [[Image Schema|image schemas]] — the pre-conceptual patterns of containment, path, force, and balance detailed in chapters 4 and 6 — as their structural scaffolding.

[[Lakoff - Women Fire and Dangerous Things|Lakoff's]] *Women, Fire, and Dangerous Things* (pp. 269-303, ch. 17) embeds conceptual metaphor within cognitive semantics, arguing that metaphorical mappings are not arbitrary but constrained by the same cognitive models that govern categorization and prototype effects. [[Kurzweil - How to Create a Mind|Kurzweil's]] Pattern Recognition Theory of Mind offers a neural substrate for this process: the hierarchically organized neocortical modules that learn patterns at successively higher levels of abstraction are, in effect, the hardware on which metaphorical mappings run. [[Hofstadter - Godel Escher Bach|Hofstadter's]] concept of isomorphism — a structure-preserving mapping between two systems — provides the formal characterization of what metaphor accomplishes cognitively. Where Lakoff and Johnson describe the phenomenon, Hofstadter supplies the mathematical intuition behind it.

Clark and Chalmers' extended cognition thesis raises a further question: if metaphorical reasoning can be offloaded onto external representations (diagrams, models, notational systems), then conceptual metaphors may persist in artifacts long after the minds that created them have dissolved. This is precisely the condition that interests [[Interdimensional Semiotics]]: meaning structures that survive the death of their original substrate.

## Significance for Interdimensional Semiotics

Conceptual metaphor is central to [[Interdimensional Semiotics]] because it is the primary mechanism by which substrate-dependent experience generates substrate-independent meaning. If abstract thought is grounded in embodied metaphor, then the semiotic gravitational mass of any concept depends on the depth and richness of the metaphorical mappings that support it. The IS question becomes: when a conceptual metaphor crosses a dimensional boundary — entering a reality with different physics, different embodiment, different sensorimotor contingencies — which components survive? The target domain (the abstract concept) may persist, but the source domain (grounded in a particular body's experience of gravity, containment, temperature) may become unintelligible.

This connects to the problem of narrative drift in [[The Noosphere]]: as meanings circulate through [[connectomes]] and are re-embodied in new substrates, their metaphorical grounding shifts. The concept UNDERSTANDING IS GRASPING presupposes hands. An intelligence without hands might preserve the target domain (comprehension) but would need to rebuild the source domain from its own embodied repertoire, producing a structurally similar but experientially alien metaphor. Tracking these mutations is one of the core tasks of interdimensional semiotic analysis.
