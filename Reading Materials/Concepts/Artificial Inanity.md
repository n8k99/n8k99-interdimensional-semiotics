# Artificial Inanity

**Artificial Inanity** is machine-mediated output that carries the surface form of intelligence, relevance, beauty, or meaning while lacking the substrate freight that would make those surfaces accountable.

The phrase is taken from [[Anathem]]'s Reticulum vocabulary and brought into IntSem as a name for a contemporary production condition: fluent systems can now generate plausible cultural material faster than cohorts can test whether the material carries lived pressure, authorship, memory, craft, or implementable structure.

## Definition

Artificial Inanity is present when:

- the artifact imitates the signs of meaningful work;
- the production process does not contain the pressure those signs normally index;
- the receiving cohort lacks, suspends, or outsources provenance discipline;
- the artifact becomes usable as filler, mood, justification, or noise;
- repeated exposure lowers the basin's tolerance for substrate-honest work.

It is not identical with AI output. AI-mediated work can carry substrate when disciplined by provenance, correction, authorship, and basin maintenance. Artificial Inanity names the failure case where the surface survives and the freight does not.

## IntSem Function

Under [[The Tinkerbell Rule]], Artificial Inanity can still become real if enough people honor it as content, strategy, culture, or taste. Under [[Reality is What is Left]], the test is harsher: subtract the honoring, and inspect whether any load-bearing substrate remains.

The danger is not merely that empty work exists. Empty work always existed. The danger is scale and polish: Artificial Inanity can flood a basin with high-fluency material that teaches participants to mistake recognition for contact.
