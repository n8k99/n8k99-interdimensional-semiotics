# States of Matter

> Minted [[2026-06-19]] as the **modern-scientific middle rung** translating the four classical alchemical elements ([[Fire]], [[Water]], [[Air]], [[Earth]]) into rigorous physics.

The **states of matter** are the qualitatively distinct phases a substance can occupy — classically **solid**, **liquid**, **gas**, and **plasma** — distinguished by the order, mobility, and binding of their constituent particles. A solid holds a fixed shape and fixed volume (particles locked in place); a liquid flows but conserves volume (particles mobile but coherent); a gas expands to fill its container (particles free and diffuse); a plasma is ionised, energetic matter mid-dissociation. The map onto the four alchemical elements is near-exact: **[[Earth]] = solid** (fixed, at rest), **[[Water]] = liquid** (mobile, conserved transport), **[[Air]] = gas** (diffuse, pervasive), **[[Fire]] = plasma** (energetic, transformation-in-progress). The pre-modern intuition that the four elements are *modes a substance can be in* rather than four distinct stuffs is precisely the modern concept of state of matter.

## Why states are sheets joined at Branch Points

Each state of matter is a **sheet**: a stable configuration a substrate can occupy, characterised by a different ordering of the same underlying particles. Moving between states is a [[Phase Transition]] across a critical point — and that critical point is a [[Branch Point]] where the state-sheets meet. At the triple point, three states are simultaneously accessible from one locus; which sheet the substrate lands on is decided by a navigational event ([[Nucleation]]) and is an act of [[Symmetry Breaking]] (the continuous symmetry of the fluid broken into the discrete order of the solid). The four elements, read as states of matter, are thus the four basic *substrate-states* of [[Substrate Materialization]] — the modes a materialized substrate can occupy on a given [[Sheet]] — and the transitions between them are branch-point crossings.

## Significance for Interdimensional Semiotics

States of matter is the rigorous hinge for the four-element row of the chart: [[Fire]]/[[Water]]/[[Air]]/[[Earth]] *are* plasma/liquid/gas/solid. This grounds the IntSem reading in which the elements are not substances but **substrate-states**: [[Earth]]-fixity (solid) is most [[Sheet|sheet]]-local, [[Air]]-diffusion (gas) most pervasive, [[Fire]] (plasma) the transformation itself, [[Water]] (liquid) the conserved-transport edge case. The fifth element [[Quintessence]] is categorically different — not a state of matter but the cross-sheet invariant orthogonal to all of them (see [[Vacuum Energy]]).
