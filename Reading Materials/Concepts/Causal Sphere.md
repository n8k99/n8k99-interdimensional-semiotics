# ⊚ Causal Sphere

## Working Definition

A **Causal Sphere** is a bounded region of [[HEMM Space]] within which a single coherent causal regime operates. Inside the sphere, the same kinds of events produce the same kinds of effects under the same kinds of conditions; the rules by which causes become effects compose without remainder. Crossing the boundary of a causal sphere puts one into a different regime where the prior causal architecture may not hold — where the cause-handles one was using to push the world go inert, and where new cause-handles, indexed to the new regime, become operative.

The Causal Sphere is the field's primary *unit-of-causal-coherence*. It is the noun that names a single, internally-consistent patch of "the way things work." [[Causal Shear]] is the verb that names what happens at its boundary.

## Why "Sphere"

The term is geometric-topological, not literal. A causal sphere is not necessarily a round region in any visualizable space. The metaphor is doing two specific kinds of work:

1. **Bounded region with an interior and a surface.** A sphere has a definite *inside* and a definite *outside*, separated by a boundary surface. This is what the concept names: a regime is *bounded* — it does not extend infinitely — and it has a *surface* across which the regime changes. The interior is where causality is coherent; the surface is where [[Causal Shear]] becomes operative; the exterior is some other sphere with some other regime.
2. **Local Euclideanness within global curvature.** In differential geometry, a manifold is locally flat — a small enough region around any point looks like ordinary Euclidean space — even when the global structure is curved or topologically non-trivial. A causal sphere is the analogous *local* structure in [[HEMM Space]]: locally the regime is uniform and ordinary coordinates apply; globally, [[HEMM Space]] is composed of many such spheres glued together by transition functions that may be highly non-trivial.

The intuition to retain: a causal sphere is a place where one can stand and do physics-or-its-equivalent without being shocked, surrounded by other places where the same procedures would not work the same way.

## Interior Structure

Not all points within a causal sphere are causally equivalent. The sphere has internal *texture*:

- **Center.** The center of a causal sphere is where the regime is most tightly coherent — the canonical instances, the textbook cases, the central exemplars. Newtonian mechanics is most thoroughly itself at the scale of falling apples and planetary orbits in the weak-field, low-velocity regime. A literary genre is most thoroughly itself in its prototypical instances. A natural language is most thoroughly itself in its most idiomatic, native-fluent productions.
- **Periphery.** Toward the boundary, the regime loosens. Edge cases appear — instances that strain the regime without quite breaking it, that require qualification, that are explained only by adding epicycles. These peripheral cases are the [[Anomaly|Kuhnian anomalies]] in their developmental phase: not yet sphere-breaking, but signaling that the sphere has limits and that those limits are being approached.
- **Density gradient.** The pattern-density (in the sense of [[Pattern Recognition]]) is highest at the center and falls off toward the periphery. A user of the regime can be confident at the center and must work harder at the periphery; the work consists of explicit reconstruction of what is implicit in the central cases.

The interior structure matters because it gives the field a way to talk about the *health* of a causal sphere without making the binary in-or-out claim. A sphere whose periphery is loaded with anomalies is approaching its limits even before the boundary itself has been reached.

## Boundary Structure

Causal spheres do not have hard walls. They have *gradient boundaries* — transition zones where the regime changes from one to another. The structure of the boundary varies:

- **Sudden transitions** are paradigm-shift seams: the [[Anomaly|anomaly]] accumulates, the old regime collapses, and the new regime takes over essentially at once for the community that has crossed. The Newtonian/relativistic boundary, viewed from the history-of-physics scale, is sudden in this sense.
- **Gradual transitions** are [[Temporal Drift]] crossings: the regime warps slowly enough across the boundary that no single shear-event registers, even though a far-enough-traversed path has clearly crossed into a different regime. A natural language's drift across centuries is a gradual sphere-boundary crossing — Old English and Modern English are different causal spheres of meaning-and-reference, but no single moment crosses the seam.
- **Standing-shear surfaces** are boundaries that remain causally active without resolving in either direction. The quantum/classical boundary is the canonical case: the two regimes coexist, the boundary remains live, and every measurement event sits on the [[Causal Shear|shear]] surface.

The boundary, in all three cases, is the locus of [[Causal Shear]]. The interior and the boundary are dual concepts: the sphere's coherence is what makes the boundary a discontinuity, and the boundary's discontinuity is what makes the sphere a bounded thing rather than an indefinitely-extended regime.

## Worked Examples Across Scales

### Cosmological

In the [[HEMM Space]] formalism, a single physical universe with its own consistent physical constants, dimensionalities, and dynamical laws is a causal sphere. The set of such spheres is the set of universes promoted to first-class status by HEMM. Crossing from one to another is sphere-crossing in the strongest possible sense: the local Euclidean chart on the destination side is a different chart, with different transition functions, and the holomorphic structure of [[HEMM Space]] determines which universes are reachable from which others. A journey like the [[EmColonizationFleet]]'s ten-year HEMM transit is a path through a sequence of causal spheres, each with its own local physics, mediated by translation machinery that adjusts the traveler's causal signature to match the local chart.

### Paradigmatic

A [[Kuhn - Structure of Scientific Revolutions|Kuhnian]] paradigm constitutes a causal sphere in the space of *scientific explanation*. Within Newtonian mechanics, a definite class of explanations works: forces, masses, accelerations, and their compositions are the cause-handles, and within the sphere they push effects reliably. Within general relativity, a different class of explanations works: stress-energy, curvature, and geodesics are the cause-handles. The paradigm boundary is a sphere boundary; what Kuhn called *incommensurability* is the recognition that the boundary is genuinely a discontinuity, not a smooth deformation.

The peripheral-anomalies pattern is central here. A paradigm in its mature decadence is one whose periphery has accumulated many edge-case anomalies that the central regime cannot quite explain — Mercury's perihelion advance, the photoelectric effect, the Michelson-Morley result. The accumulation is the sphere announcing its boundary.

### Disciplinary

Each academic discipline operates as a causal sphere within the larger space of inquiry. Within economics, certain explanations push: prices, incentives, equilibria, market-clearing. Within evolutionary biology, different explanations push: selection pressure, fitness gradients, drift. The same phenomenon (say, cooperation) can be addressed from inside either sphere, and the explanations will not be commensurable in any straightforward way — they index different causal architectures.

Cross-disciplinary work is sphere-crossing work. The interdisciplinary researcher's labour is exactly the labour of constructing transition functions between two causal spheres that nominally address the same territory but causally do not. *The friction characteristic of interdisciplinary writing — the sense that nothing quite lands the way it should — is the local geometry of the shear surface between the disciplinary spheres.* IntSem itself is a deliberate act of sphere-positioning: founding a discipline whose subject matter is precisely the cross-sphere translation operations that other disciplines treat as either trivial or impossible.

### Linguistic

Each natural language constitutes a causal sphere of meaning-and-reference. Within the language, signs co-refer reliably, idioms compose, jokes land. Outside, none of this is guaranteed. Translation is sphere-crossing labour: the translator constructs a transition function from one local chart to another, and the translation succeeds to the degree that the function approximates the identity on the [[Topological Invariant|invariant]] content of the message.

Some content is invariant and crosses cleanly (numerical claims, factual descriptions, simple imperatives). Some content is sphere-indexed and does not cross (puns, register-specific tone, untranslatable affect-words). The translator's working theory of which is which is, implicitly, a working theory of the topology of the boundary between the two linguistic spheres.

### Personal

Each person operates inside their own causal sphere of narrative-and-expectation. Within one's own sphere, certain things mean certain things, certain events propel certain reactions, certain symbols have certain weight. From inside, this regime feels like *the way things are*. From outside — to another person — it is one regime among many, with its own characteristic signatures and its own characteristic blind spots.

Intimate relationship is sphere-crossing operation between two persons' spheres, sustained over time. The relationship's stability depends on both parties' tolerance for the local geometry of the boundary between their spheres — for the inevitable [[Causal Shear|shear]] events when one party's cause-handles fail to push the other party's effects, when a gesture meant in one regime lands in another regime as something quite different. Long relationships are, in this framing, long-running collaborative projects of building transition functions between two evolving causal spheres.

### AI-Model

A trained model's effective causal sphere is *the training distribution plus the regions reachable by interpolation within its representational geometry*. Inside this sphere, the model's behavior is coherent: features push toward responses the way they did in training, the model's voice is stable, the cause-handles its developers have learned to use actually move the outputs.

Out-of-distribution inputs cross the sphere boundary. The three failure modes catalogued in [[Causal Shear|Causal Shear]] — inert causes, effects without training-frame causes, causal inversions — are the local geometry of the boundary surface. Hallucinations, distribution-shift failures, and jailbreaks are not separate phenomena; they are the same phenomenon viewed through different operational lenses, each of them being the failure of the model's causal sphere to extend to the input under consideration.

The IntSem-relevant question for AI safety is then: *how do we characterize a model's causal sphere ahead of deployment, so that we know when we have crossed its boundary?* Capability evaluations, viewed through this frame, are attempts to map the sphere's interior; red-teaming and adversarial probing are attempts to map its boundary. The current discourse circles this question without naming it. Naming it sharpens what eval methodologies are actually doing and what they are systematically missing.

### Genre

A literary genre is a causal sphere in narrative-space (developed at length in [[Causal Shear]]'s Narrative section). Within a tragedy, certain events cause certain consequences; within a farce, the same events cause different consequences. The genre boundary is a sphere boundary, and adaptations across genres are sphere-crossing operations subject to all the same translation constraints as cross-linguistic or cross-paradigmatic work.

## Causal Sphere vs. Sheet

The [[Sheet|sheet]] and the causal sphere are close concepts but not identical, and the distinction is load-bearing:

- A [[Sheet|sheet]] is defined by the multi-valued function resolving to a single value across a connected region of the manifold — it is a *global* property, the surface picked out by following one branch of the function consistently.
- A **causal sphere** is defined by *local* causal coherence — a single coordinate patch on the manifold within which the regime is uniform.

A sheet is typically composed of many overlapping causal spheres, glued together by transition functions. Two inhabitants of the same sheet may live in different causal spheres and only discover they are sheet-mates by traversing the transitions between them. Sheets are the large-scale structure of [[HEMM Space]]; causal spheres are the local physics neighbourhoods within those sheets.

## Relationship to Causal Shear

[[Causal Shear]] is what happens *at* the boundary between causal spheres, or *across* the surface of a single sphere when internal stresses force the sphere to fracture. The two concepts are dual:

- **Causal Sphere** is the noun: a unit of causal coherence, a bounded region with internal structure and a boundary.
- **Causal Shear** is the operation: the discontinuity in the causal-relation field at the boundary surface, the mismatch that prevents one sphere's causes from pushing into another sphere's effect-space.

There can be no shear without spheres on either side of it; there can be no spheres without shear surfaces bounding them. The pair is co-defining, in the same way that an edge requires two regions and a region requires its bounding edges.

The within-system framing of shear (developed in [[Causal Shear]]'s HEMM-era section) is exactly the case where the boundary between two causal spheres runs *through* an entity rather than between entities. The entity straddles the surface; parts are on one side, parts on the other; the entity's internal causality fractures because its parts are in different spheres.

## Relationship to CIF

Each causal sphere has a [[CIF (Cosmic Index Factor)|CIF]]-address — its position in [[HEMM Space]]. The topological structure of HEMM determines which spheres are *adjacent* to which others, and therefore which sphere-crossings are possible at all from any given starting position. Some sphere-pairs are nearby in CIF terms (small, low-shear transitions); others are far apart (large, high-shear transitions or no direct transition at all).

This gives the field a way to make *comparative* claims about translation difficulty. The translation difficulty between two spheres is not arbitrary — it is determined by the CIF-distance between them and by the local topology of the manifold between their charts. Some seeming cousins (e.g., closely-related natural languages) are CIF-near; some seeming siblings (e.g., theories that share vocabulary across disciplinary boundaries) are CIF-far despite the surface similarity.

## Relationship to Branch Points

[[Branch Point|Branch points]] in [[HEMM Space]] are the loci where multiple causal spheres meet. Navigating a branch point is choosing which sphere to enter — committing to one regime among the several that converge at that point. The branch point itself is, in a sense, a degenerate region where no single sphere is yet operative; the act of crossing it consists of the resolution into one definite sphere on the far side.

The decision-points in lived practice that feel weighty out of proportion to their immediate stakes are, often, branch points in the local sphere-topology: choices that commit to one causal regime over others not because the immediate consequences are large, but because the sphere being entered determines which causes will push in everything that follows. *The biographical "fork-in-the-road" moments are branch points at the personal-sphere scale.*

## Application to AI

Returning to the AI case for sustained treatment, because it is where the concept becomes operationally measurable in a contemporary way:

A trained model's effective causal sphere has a definite, in-principle-mappable interior and a definite, in-principle-mappable boundary. Three distinct mapping problems present themselves:

1. **Interior characterization.** What is the central region of the sphere — the inputs on which the model is most reliably itself? Standard benchmarks are attempts at this, though they typically sample the central region without characterizing its full extent.
2. **Boundary characterization.** Where does the sphere end? Adversarial probing, OOD detection methods, and red-teaming all probe the boundary, but rarely produce a coherent map of it. The boundary is treated as a set of local failure modes rather than as a global topological feature.
3. **Sphere-deployment matching.** Given a deployment territory (the actual distribution of inputs the model will encounter in production), does the model's causal sphere cover the territory? This is the question the field's safety conversations are circling. A model whose sphere is smaller than its deployment territory will produce shear at the uncovered regions, and the failures will appear at exactly the places where the deployment territory exits the sphere.

The IntSem reframe of AI safety: *the central object of analysis is the causal sphere of the deployed system, and the central question is the geometry of the sphere relative to the geometry of the deployment territory.* Capability evaluation, alignment work, and safety testing are all, on this framing, sub-disciplines of causal-sphere mapping.

## Significance for IntSem

Causal Sphere is the field's primary *unit-of-causal-coherence* concept. Most IntSem analysis assumes a causal-sphere structure even when not naming it — assumes that there is a "regime" within which a phenomenon is being analyzed, and that crossing out of that regime means crossing into something the analysis must treat differently. Making the sphere structure explicit clarifies several things at once:

- It enables *comparative* claims: this paradigm's sphere is larger than that one's; this language's sphere overlaps significantly with that one's; this model's sphere is too small for the deployment territory it has been placed into.
- It enables *boundary-locating* claims: the shear is between *these two* spheres, at *this* surface; the anomalies cluster *here* in the sphere's periphery; the translation friction is concentrated at *that* boundary feature.
- It enables *interior-structural* claims: this regime is most itself *here*, at this center; it loosens *there*, toward this periphery; the canonical exemplars sit *here*, the strained edge cases *there*.

Without the explicit sphere structure, IntSem analyses tend toward either over-generalization (treating all regimes as one) or fragmentation (treating each phenomenon in isolation). The sphere concept is the middle term that makes the field's characteristic comparative work possible.

The methodological posture is the same as for [[Causal Shear]]: the IntSem researcher's job is not to dissolve sphere-boundaries into one universal regime, nor to retreat into the local sphere as if it were the whole world, but to *map* the sphere structure. Where are the spheres? What are their interiors like? What are their boundaries like? What is the local topology of [[HEMM Space]] in the neighbourhood of the spheres of interest? The map of the sphere structure is the working geography of the field.

## Open Development

- **Sphere-size metric.** What is the natural measure of a causal sphere's extent? Candidates include the volume of input space on which the regime is coherent, the dimension of the invariant ring over the sphere, or some integral of pattern-density across the interior. None is yet developed.
- **Adjacency topology.** The claim that some sphere-pairs are CIF-near and others CIF-far needs formalization. What is the metric on the space of spheres? Is it inherited from the HEMM manifold, or does it require its own construction?
- **Empirical sphere-mapping protocol for AI.** The most operationally tractable case. What would it look like to *produce* a map of a deployed model's causal sphere — both interior and boundary — in advance of deployment? Connects to current eval methodology and to the [[Causal Shear]] open-development item on shear measurement.
- **The personal-sphere case in detail.** The claim that intimate relationships are sphere-crossing operations is gestural in this pass. A worked treatment connecting to [[Symbolic Dissonance]] and to attachment-theoretic frames is wanted.
