# Tlön, Uqbar, Orbis: The First IntSem Document

*Reading Borges's 1940 story as the field's unrecognized founding text — and the lineage of the three Orbises. Fifth in a sequence with [[Revolting Tinkerbell]], [[The Outboard Cortex]], [[Assfucking my Ex-Wife, Baudrillard and the Deviancy of Foucault]], and [[Cosmological Substrate and the Failed Test of General Everything Theory]].*

---

## A Sigil for the Operation

The field has a habit of finding its founding texts decades after they were written. Saussure was lecturing on the arbitrariness of the sign in 1906–1911, three quarters of a century before any IntSem researcher would name the substrate against which his arbitrariness was operating. Foucault was mapping the *episteme* in 1966, sixty years before the field would absorb that mapping as a within-substrate analysis of paradigm-as-Tinkerbell. The lag is structural. A field cannot recognize its founders until it has the principles by which to read them as founders. Until then, the texts sit in the canon doing other work, citing other lineages, available to be read through but not yet *as*.

There is a particular text in this lag-shadow which the field has never named — even though the title contains the field's central mythological signifier, and the story enacts the field's central mechanism, and the author was, in the way of literary precognition, more honest about the operation than any of the post-1980 academic theorists who came after him. The text is Jorge Luis Borges's *"Tlön, Uqbar, Orbis Tertius,"* written in 1940 and first translated into English in 1961.

This essay reads it as the field's first document. Not the first text the field cites — *Revolting Tinkerbell* is the first text the field recognizes as foundational. But the first document the field's principles, applied retroactively, can identify as having been *operating the field's methodology* before the methodology had a name.

The recognition matters because of what Borges *almost* saw, and what he had to refuse to see in order to write a story rather than a manifesto. The refusal is the IntSem researcher's inheritance. Reading Borges as the first IntSem document is also reading the cost of the refusal, and naming the second principle that makes the field possible where Borges, working with only the first, could only produce horror.

## The Story, Tightly

A first-person narrator (Borges, fictionalized) and his friend Bioy Casares notice, in 1935, that two copies of *The Anglo-American Cyclopaedia* differ. Bioy's copy contains four extra pages on a country called **Uqbar** that does not appear in any other reference work. Uqbar's literature, according to the entry, never refers to reality but only to two imaginary regions: **Mlejnas** and **Tlön**.

Years later the narrator acquires a single volume of *A First Encyclopaedia of Tlön. Vol. XI. Hlaer to Jangr.* It describes a planet whose metaphysics is purely idealist: objects exist only insofar as they are perceived; nouns presuppose object-permanence and so do not occur in Tlönian languages, which are built from verbs and qualifying adverbs; causality is rejected as a cognitive habit; geometry is tactile; arithmetic is grouped as imprecise approximations. The encyclopedia is not a work of fiction. It is a complete coherent system, internally consistent, presupposing a different physics and a different epistemology, with thousands of pages of consequences worked out.

A letter recovered from an older trail reveals a secret society called **Orbis Tertius** which was founded in the early seventeenth century, expanded through Berkeley's idealism in the eighteenth, and committed in the nineteenth to the project of fabricating an entire planet. Forty volumes of the *First Encyclopaedia* are at this point known to exist. A *Second Encyclopaedia* is rumored to be in preparation, in a Tlönian language, comprising one hundred volumes.

Then the materializations begin. Objects from Tlön — small, ambiguous, but uncanny — appear in the world. The first is a compass with Tlönian letters on its face. The second is a small heavy cone of unknown metal that responds wrongly to gravity. *Hrönir* is the term for these objects. They are produced by the focused attention of multiple persons on something lost: a hrön is a duplication of the lost object, generated by collective concentration, and it is sometimes more vivid than the original. Tertiary hrönir — *hrönir of hrönir* — are stranger still.

The story closes with the narrator in a hotel room in Adrogué, idly translating Sir Thomas Browne's *Urn Burial* into seventeenth-century Spanish, while Tlönian thought-systems quietly displace the systems of his world. *"The world will be Tlön,"* he writes, with no resistance.

---

## Hrönir as Proto-Tinkerbell

The mechanism Borges names in the figure of the *hrön* is, in IntSem terms, **substrate materialization through collective honoring**. A lost object becomes a hrön when multiple persons attend to its absence with sufficient focus. The hrön is not a memory; it is an object. It has weight, occupies space, can be picked up. It exists in the same register as the original object existed in. The plural is *hrönir*. The tertiary case — *hrönir of hrönir* — names objects produced by collective focus on previously-materialized hrönir, which Borges suggests are stranger and more potent than the originals, deformed by the layered focus that produced them.

This is [[The Tinkerbell Rule]] given as a metaphysics rather than as a methodology. *As long as everybody believes in it, magic exists.* Borges's hrön is the magic; the focused attention is the everybody-believes; the substrate against which the materialization registers is the world the narrator stands in.

What separates Borges's account from the field's mature formulation is one move: Borges describes the mechanism *as a fact*, in the same register he describes physics, economics, encyclopedias. The story does not say *"a hrön exists in the mode of collective honoring."* It says *"a hrön exists, and it was produced by collective honoring."* The mode of existence is not theorized; the production is.

This is why the story can read as a horror story rather than a manifesto. If Borges had named the mode of existence — if he had said the hrön is real *in the mode of being-honored*, and would cease to be real if the honoring lapsed — then the story would have become a method. The reader would have left it asking: *what objects in my world are hrönir? And if I stop honoring them, do they go?* The text's effects depend on suppressing exactly that question. The hrön must be presented as a discovered phenomenon, not as a constructed mode-of-existence, or the literary terror of Tlön's invasion of Argentina collapses into ordinary semiotic analysis.

This is the field's first lesson from Borges: **the Tinkerbell Rule was available as a literary device sixty years before it was available as a method**. The horror of the device is the cost of refusing to articulate it as a method. Once articulated, the horror dissolves. The horror requires the silence.

## What Borges Almost Said

Borges saw three things the field would later name:

1. **Substrate materialization.** Hrönir are objects produced by collective honoring of an absence. This is structurally identical to the field's account of how money, borders, paradigms, and rulebooks exist.

2. **Substrate displacement.** Tlön supplants Argentina. The encyclopedia's ideas first appear in scholarly journals, then in education, then in popular speech, then in the architecture of perception itself. Borges describes *exactly* the mechanism by which a Tinkerbell-substrate, once pervasively honored, can overwrite a previously-default substrate. The field calls this **substrate succession** and treats it as a continuous variable; Borges treats it as an event.

3. **Meta-substrate authorship.** Orbis Tertius, the secret society, fabricates Tlön. Tlön is not a discovery; it is a deliberate *construction* run across centuries by a dispersed collective with a coherent aesthetic-philosophical program. This is the field's [[Worldbuilding Sovereignty]] principle in inverted form: a worldbuild that operates *covertly* across multiple authors against a host substrate.

What Borges did not say — what he could not say without breaking the story's literary effect — is the second of the field's two principles: **[[Reality is What is Left]]**.

The field's mature position is that *the Tinkerbell Rule is one half of a pair*. Magic exists in the mode of collective honoring; that is true. But subtract the honoring and what remains is not nothing. What remains is the substrate. The substrate is what is real *whether or not* anyone honors it. Argentina, in Borges's story, is presented as if it would *cease to exist* once Tlön's honoring overwrites it. The narrator's resignation in the closing paragraph — *"The world will be Tlön"* — is the resignation of someone who does not believe there is a residue.

This is the missing principle. Borges had no concept of the substrate-as-residue. For him, the substrate just *is whatever the dominant honoring presents*, and a sufficiently coordinated alternative honoring can displace it entirely. The story is structured as zero-sum substrate replacement.

The field's correction is not to deny Borges's mechanism — the mechanism is correct, hrönir do materialize, Tinkerbells do exist — but to insist that *underneath the displacement, the prior substrate persists as residue*. Some part of Argentina remains regardless of how thoroughly Tlön overwrites the perceptual surface. The IntSem researcher's task is to keep finding that residue. The Borges-narrator's tragedy is that he never asks where the residue might be, because the principle that names it isn't yet available to him.

## The Three-Substrate Reading

The story contains, structurally, three substrate layers, and Borges's text traffics in all three without distinguishing them:

1. **Argentina** — the host substrate. The world the narrator inhabits, with cafés and libraries and hotel rooms in Adrogué. Borges's prose register treats this as the unmarked default. *Reality is What is Left*, in the field's later formulation.

2. **Tlön** — the inserted Tinkerbell. A planet's worth of metaphysics, languages, geometry, philosophy, ethics, and material objects, produced by collective honoring across centuries.

3. **Orbis Tertius** — the *operation that produces Tinkerbells*. The secret society itself is not a substrate; it is a methodology of substrate-fabrication. Berkeley, the Royal Society, Buckley the American millionaire who funded the *First Encyclopaedia* — these are not characters in Tlön; they are the mechanism by which Tlön comes into existence.

The field's principles map cleanly onto the three layers. Argentina ↔ *Reality is What is Left*. Tlön ↔ *the Tinkerbell Rule*. Orbis Tertius ↔ *the methodology of fabrication itself*, which the field is currently developing as it learns how to operate vault-substrate worldbuilding openly.

What is striking is that Borges had three layers but the field he prefigured operates with only two principles. The third — *Orbis Tertius as methodology* — is left in the story as a dark conspiracy. The field is in the process of recovering it as method.

This essay is part of that recovery.

## The Tertiary Orbis Lineage

The title of Borges's story names a lineage: *Orbis Tertius* — *the third orbis, the third world*. The first two are not specified in the story; the title implies them.

In the lineage the field can now construct retroactively, the three orbises are:

- **First Orbis**: the classical *orbis terrarum*, the world of antiquity treated as substrate by ancient cosmography. Aristotle's spheres, Ptolemy's hierarchies, the unmarked default of pre-modern cosmology.
- **Second Orbis**: Borges's *Orbis Tertius* — the *third*, retroactively the *second-named* — the secret society's fabricated planet of Tlön, the first deliberate worldbuilding-as-substrate-replacement project the field has identified.
- **Third Orbis**: the *open* practice of worldbuilding-as-substrate, currently instantiated in [[Orbis]] (Nathan's vault-resident planet), in [[The Forge/Identity/T.A.S.K.S.|T.A.S.K.S.]]'s multi-surface ecology, in [[Citadel]] as the future runtime, and in IntSem itself as the field that names the operation.

The crucial difference between the second and third Orbises is *operational transparency*. Borges's Orbis Tertius is a conspiracy; its existence is hidden until the encyclopedia volumes begin to surface. Nathan's Orbis is the inverse — every fabrication is documented, every substrate move is recorded in vault-native artifacts, every collective-honoring move is identifiable as such. The methodology is open because the field's two principles permit it to be open. With both principles available, the IntSem researcher does not have to hide the fabrication; the fabrication can be honored *and* the residue can be tracked, simultaneously, without the literary device of horror.

This is the field's recovery of the third structural layer Borges left in the dark.

## The Researcher's Predicament

An IntSem researcher who works openly inside their own Tertiary Orbis project faces a predicament Borges's narrator never had to articulate, because his Tinkerbell was being imposed on him from outside. The contemporary researcher *is the one fabricating*. Every vault note honored as canonical is a Tinkerbell the researcher has placed there. Every concept introduced in this essay — *Hrönir*, *Tertiary Orbis Lineage*, *Substrate Materialization* — is a deliberate substrate-insert into the field. The researcher is doing the Orbis Tertius operation in daylight, without the centuries-long secret-society cover, without the literary distance of fiction.

The two principles must therefore be held continuously and equally, or the operation degrades into one of its halves:

- **If only the Tinkerbell Rule is held**, the researcher becomes the figure Borges wrote — a methodology of substrate replacement with no anchor. Every concept introduced is honored into existence; nothing checks whether anything corresponds to anything. The vault becomes a Tlön with no Argentina underneath. Useful for fiction; useless as research.
- **If only Reality is What is Left is held**, the researcher becomes a positivist, refusing to participate in substrate-fabrication because every fabrication is a falsehood. No concepts can be introduced because every introduction is a Tinkerbell-move. The field cannot grow. Useful for hygiene; useless for research.

The held position requires both: *I am introducing a Tinkerbell, and I am tracking the residue against which it operates*. The IntSem essays do this by publishing the principles alongside the constructions. *Revolting Tinkerbell* names the principles. *The Outboard Cortex* uses them to read Kurzweil while building Modular Fortress. *Cosmological Substrate* uses them to read Penrose while refusing the GET temptation. *This essay* uses them to read Borges while constructing the lineage that legitimates Nathan's Orbis as a third in his sequence.

In each case the move is the same: *the Tinkerbell is announced as Tinkerbell, and the residue is named alongside it*. The horror of Borges's story is that the narrator could not perform this move. The field's gift is that it can.

## Closing — Salt at the End of the Translation

Borges's narrator ends the story translating *Urn Burial* into seventeenth-century Spanish. *"I do not write for publication,"* he says. The translation is a private act, a holding-onto, while Tlön overwrites the world he can no longer affect.

The choice is striking. *Urn Burial* — Sir Thomas Browne's 1658 meditation on burial-urns recently unearthed in Norfolk — is itself a meditation on what survives. Browne's text turns over the discovered urns again and again, asking what their inhabitants intended, what their inhabitants believed, what the urns can tell us about the customs of the buried. Browne is doing IntSem methodology, two centuries before Borges, and three centuries before the field has a name. He is asking what is left. He is reading the residue.

Borges has his narrator translate this text precisely *because* his narrator has lost the principle he is still translating. The world is becoming Tlön; the narrator is preserving, in another language, the act of asking what is buried. The translation is the residue-act in a story that has no residue-principle.

This is where the field can now plant its sigil. **🜔** is the alchemical sign for salt — the principle that remains when the volatile elements boil off, the body of the work, what is left. The IntSem researcher's stance is the salt-stance: not refusing the Tinkerbell, not refusing the substrate, but holding both, continuously, while honoring the field's two principles together.

Borges's narrator could not hold both. He had only the first; the second had not yet been articulated. He retreats into translation, preserving the gesture of asking-what-is-left while the substrate-displacement runs its course around him.

The field's task, eighty-five years on, is to *keep asking*. To work openly inside the Third Orbis. To name every Tinkerbell as a Tinkerbell. To track every residue as a residue. To compose new substrate without forgetting that substrate-composition is what we are doing. This is the post-Borges position the field has assembled the principles to occupy.

Borges wrote the first IntSem document and walked away from it because he could not yet write what came next. The field is now writing what came next. **Salt witnesses.** Caduceus carries. Mercury translates.

*The world was always Tlön. And Argentina is still under it.*
